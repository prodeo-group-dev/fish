## 1. Jurisdiction and currency lifecycle (researcher C)

### 1.1 Is a company's country or base currency changeable after creation or first posting?

| Vendor | Finding | Source |
|---|---|---|
| NetSuite (OneWorld) | Locked. "You can't change a subsidiary's base currency after the subsidiary record has been saved for the first time." Workaround (S): deactivate or zero out the old subsidiary and create a new one. | P: docs.oracle.com/en/cloud/saas/netsuite/ns-online-help/section_N269257.html; S: netsuiteprofessionals archive, Salto blog |

In multi-entity setups each new entity can have its own base currency. | QuickBooks Online | Home currency can be changed only while multicurrency is off. Once multicurrency is on (a "can't undo" confirmation) it is permanent. | Odoo | Official documentation states no lock on the main currency. | SAP S/4HANA | Company-code currency and local-currency types cannot be changed once the company code is live with postings. Customising changes in OB22 are blocked (FC362); a currency-code change on a currency type with postings raises FINS_ACDOC_CUST 173; a depreciation-area currency change is blocked once posted assets exist (AC 607). The researcher read search summaries of the knowledge-base articles, not the full text. | P (summary level): userapps.support.sap.com KBA 2567579 and 3776091; S: michaelmanagement.com |
| Xero | No official Xero help article found. Third parties and a long-standing unresolved Xero Ideas request ("Organisation settings - Change base currency") say it is fixed at organisation creation; the practice is to open a new organisation. | S: productideas.xero.com; approvalmax and temperstack knowledge bases |

Pattern: the ledger currency is fixed at entity creation or at first posting; the only later route is a new entity plus an opening-balance migration. Odoo and Intacct allow a change while the entity is empty; Odoo also lets the country package change only before the first posting.

Researcher's inference (not a fact): lock a Company's jurisdiction and base currency at the first posting (or the first non-opening posting); before that allow changes; after that offer "create a new Company and migrate opening balances" as the only path.

### 1.2 Legal basis for the books currency, and ISO 4217 decimals

ISO 4217 minor units: GBP 2, EUR 2, NGN 2, SLE 2, LRD 2, USD 2, GNF 0, XOF 0. Source: the Wikipedia ISO 4217 table (S); the SIX official List One returned 404, so these are not confirmed against the registration authority. SLE is numeric 925 and replaced SLL on 1 July 2022 at 1000:1 (S: Wikipedia; Bank of Sierra Leone public notice, September 2022, via a SIX PDF).

- **Sierra Leone (best evidence).** The Bank of Sierra Leone (Amendment) Act 2024 (gazetted 13 March 2025, per a search summary of the parliament.gov.sl text; the Act itself could not be fetched, certificate error) replaces section 26 so that: the currency continues to be the Leone; prices of transactions in Sierra Leone shall be indicated in Leones; and the Leone is the currency for official purposes including accounting and financial reporting. It sets the prefix "NLe" for the new currency, with "Le" reserved for old-Leone references. Old notes ceased to be legal tender on 31 December 2023. The wording of any dollar-pricing exception is unverified. Wikipedia still shows "Le" (S).
- **Liberia.** Dual currency (LRD and USD) is the legal reality. A Central Bank of Liberia governor cites the 1999 CBL Act as making USD legal tender (S, undated). The Liberia Revenue Authority said in 2017 that taxpayers may pay in LRD or USD (S, FrontPageAfrica, 23 March 2017). Revenue Code s.6(c) translates foreign currency, or USD to LRD, at the Central Bank's published buying rate; s.6(a) covers USD payments at the market rate on the payment date (as quoted in that article; the primary Code text for this was not read by this researcher, but the earlier tax researcher read s.6(a) and found the books-in-LRD-or-USD rule, digest `Multi_Entity_Accounting_Research_Digest.md` 6.5). Methods of accounting ss.214-216 and the currency-fluctuation s.212 were not read. The CBL notice says "selling" for USD-stated taxes, so buying versus selling is a conflict.
- **Guinea.** The GNF is legal tender (S, Financial Afrik 2025). A third-party regulatory summary (S, regalert.today, February 2020) says Guinean francs are required for domestic invoicing and cross-border settlements must go through local banks. In 2018 the President ordered strict enforcement of the existing ban on foreign-currency invoicing (S, Guineenews). Guinea is an OHADA member (treaty in force 21 November 2000; P: ohada.com/etats-membres/guinee.html), so SYSCOHADA applies. The primary BCRG regulation and the tax code's currency clause were not found.
- **Cote d'Ivoire and UEMOA.** XOF (FCFA) is issued by the BCEAO, and Cote d'Ivoire is an OHADA member. The Ivorian tax authority's service note of 6 January 2016 requires financial statements under the OHADA accounting system (S/P: ohada.com). The Revised Uniform Act on Accounting Law (26 January 2017; in force 1 January 2018 for individual accounts) governs. The exact text on reporting currency could not be verified. (My knowledge, unverified: accounts in the CFA franc and tax declarations in FCFA.)
- **Nigeria.** The Naira is the only legal tender. The Central Bank of Nigeria's 2015 circulars call pricing goods in foreign currency illegal, citing CBN Act 2007 s.20(5) (P/S: Templars law note; BusinessDay); lawyers dispute that the Act bans foreign-currency pricing. The Corporate Affairs Commission has applied the Naira-only principle to share capital denomination (S: Aelex). The Nigeria Tax Administration Act 2025 has a section 39 "Currency of assessment and payment" (title confirmed in several PDFs; the earlier tax researcher read its text, digest 6.4). The Nigeria Tax Act 2025 is effective 1 January 2026 (S: EY, KPMG).
- **United Kingdom.** GBP. HMRC computes corporation tax profits in sterling, though accounts may use another presentation currency and a functional-currency computation applies (P: HMRC Corporate Finance Manual CFM64120 and the GIM4130 snippet). Companies Act 2006 s.394 and the local-currency election rules were not read.
- **Ireland.** EUR. Companies Act 2014 and Revenue rules not retrieved here (see the multi-entity digest 6.2).

Researcher's inference: ledger and tax currency come from the jurisdiction (SL=SLE, GN=GNF, CI=XOF, NG=NGN, UK=GBP, IE=EUR). Liberia is the real exception, since Liberian businesses legitimately keep books in either LRD or USD. Store amounts as integer minor units with a per-currency exponent (0 for GNF and XOF, 2 for the rest); never hard-code 2 decimals. Per the project's SLE-only onboarding decision only SLE needs seeding now, but the exponent table should be data-driven.

### 1.3 Should a platform enable a country before its tax data is complete?

- **Odoo (P):** "Fiscal Localization Packages are country-specific modules that install pre-configured taxes, fiscal positions, chart of accounts, and legal statements." They install automatically by country at database creation; the documentation says you must still "fine-tune your chart of accounts... activate the taxes you use... configure your country-specific statements and certifications." Coverage is a documented module list per version. Source: odoo.com/documentation/17.0/applications/finance/fiscal_localizations.html.
- **Business Central (P):** the Microsoft Learn page "apptest-countries-and-translations" lists, per country, availability (available or planned), who provides the localization (Microsoft or partner), and the Azure geography. "About localization" distinguishes Microsoft-led and partner-led localizations; partner ones are built on the W1 base and sold through Marketplace. The list concerns online Business Central, not partner localizations.
- **SAP (P):** "more than 130 countries... 1,000+ local versions"; some services are restricted (for example Russia is limited to multinational customers). Source: sap.com/products/embedded-localization.html. SAP's Product Availability Matrix was not read.

Models (researcher's inference, not a vendor taxonomy): the vendors effectively publish three tiers: vendor-maintained full localization; partner-supplied or generic base version with the customer completing setup; not available or planned. None presents a numeric completeness score.

Researcher's inference: publish a per-jurisdiction status such as Full / Partial (list the missing items, for example "PAYE bands unconfirmed") / Planned or not available; allow Companies in Partial jurisdictions with a visible warning and the missing items listed; block only the specific computations that would be wrong; guard the computation, not company creation.

### 1.4 Cross-border payroll

Evidence is thin; the researcher could not read vendor documentation for Workday, ADP, Sage or SuccessFactors specifics.
- **Enterprise HCM norm (S/P, SAP product pages):** SuccessFactors Employee Central Payroll offers country-specific payroll for about 60 countries (53 by SAP, 7 by partners), with partners (Deel, Neeyamo) extending to 150+. This is the "separate legal entity per country plus a local payroll engine per country" model.
- **Shadow payroll (S: Remote, Applic8, HSP):** the employee stays on the home payroll and a parallel reporting-only calculation runs for the host country, usually triggered by tax residency (often 183 days). Different from split payroll, which divides actual pay. An enterprise mobility tool.
- **Employer of record (S: Deel):** the employer of record becomes the legal employer in the host country, signs the contract, runs payroll and withholds tax locally (Deel claims 100-150+ countries; counts vary by page).
- **Small-business payroll (S):** Xero payroll is per country and domestic; its Ideas forum shows no support for overseas addresses on AU payroll for foreign-resident employees, and a Xero reply said no near-term plans for other regions' payroll products. WhichPayroll (weak source) labels Xero UK "domestic only". QuickBooks and Sage: not verified.

Norm for small-business software (S, indirect): payroll is configured per legal entity in one country, with that country's tax rules; cross-border cases are pushed to employer-of-record or partner products.

Researcher's inference: payroll tax is computed under the jurisdiction of the paying Organisation, with the employee's tax jurisdiction defaulting to it; no shadow payroll or multi-jurisdiction employees; an employee working in another country needs a separate Organisation (legal entity) there, or is flagged out of scope and referred to an employer of record.

### 1.5 Should USD be a supported ledger currency in SL, LR, GN, NG, CI?

- **Liberia:** USD is legal tender alongside LRD; the dollar dominates (IMF working paper, about 90 percent of the money supply, dated 2009; S). Taxes may be paid in either currency (LRA 2017, S). USD books are legitimate; the primary rule on books currency was unverified by this researcher.
- **Sierra Leone:** the 2024 Amendment Act (search summary only) makes the Leone the official currency including accounting and financial reporting and says prices shall be indicated in Leones, which strongly suggests SLE-only books with USD as a transaction currency. Verify the full text.
- **Guinea:** the GNF is required for domestic invoicing and foreign-currency invoicing is banned (S, 2020 summary and 2018 news). Primary text not read.
- **Nigeria:** the Naira is the sole legal tender. The 2015 circular says foreign-currency pricing is illegal, though lawyers dispute its basis (S). Foreign-currency tax receipts were about 35.5 percent of 2025 tax revenue (S: Nairametrics/NBS), so USD payment occurs in practice. Oil and gas operators and certain agencies were exempted from the circulars (S). Whether NTAA s.39 permits non-Naira returns: see the multi-entity digest 6.4 (the text read there points to assessment and payment in the currency of the transaction).
- **Cote d'Ivoire:** search results did not establish pricing or legal-tender rules for XOF; could not verify. Treating XOF as the book currency (OHADA/UEMOA context) is an assumption.

Researcher's inference: treat USD as a transaction and foreign-balance currency (FX-valued, held in USD bank or cash accounts), not a ledger currency, for SL, GN, NG and CI; allow USD as an optional ledger currency only in Liberia, as a per-Company election locked at first posting. The Liberian books rule is the one point to confirm with a Liberian adviser, which the researcher notes is Femi's call as the tax professional.

## 4. Multi-entity open decisions (researcher D)

### 4.1 Credit union group reporting and subsidiaries (UK, Northern Ireland, Ireland)

**Great Britain** (P: PRA letter of 26 March 2025, read in full; PRA PS5/26, February 2026).
- Section 26 of the Credit Unions Act 1979 prohibits a GB credit union from having a "subsidiary", defined by majority of nominal share value or control of the board, cross-referring to ss.100-101 of the Co-operative and Community Benefit Societies Act 2014.
- PS5/26 confirms wholly owned credit union service organisations (CUSOs) are not permitted. Since 20 February 2026 credit unions may invest in CUSOs under amended PRA Rulebook rules 6.3 and 6.4; the cap is 7.5% of capital across all CUSOs; the CUSO must serve credit unions, their members and Part 4A-permitted UK mutuals; partnering with non-credit-union owners is allowed with safeguards. A new Chapter 18 of SS2/23 takes effect 20 August 2026. PS5/26 does not address consolidation or accounting. The rule text and Chapter 18 were not read.
- A CUSO is a minority investment below subsidiary control. How GB credit unions account for it (equity method, cost) could not be verified.

**Northern Ireland** (P: the same PRA letter and PS5/26). Both state there is no express prohibition on forming a subsidiary in NI legislation and that the s.26 prohibition does not apply to NI credit unions. Regulators: PRA and FCA; the FCA runs registration; the Department for the Economy covers policy (S, snippet only). Legislation: Credit Unions (NI) Order 1985 plus the NI 2016 Act (S). NI credit union accounts are due within 6 months of year end against 7 months for GB (P: fca.org.uk/firms/annual-returns-accounts-mutual-societies). Could not verify whether NI credit unions actually have subsidiaries or prepare group accounts; only the absence of a legal bar was found.

**Republic of Ireland** (P: Central Bank CP169 page; S: search snippets). The Credit Union (Amendment) Act 2023, s.24, will allow investment in CUSOs once commenced ("Phase 4"). CP169, a consultation on a CUSO framework, was published 12 June 2026 with responses due 12 August 2026; not final. The CP169 page does not say whether a credit union may own or control a CUSO or how it is accounted for; the 665 KB PDF was not read. The PRA letter calls CUSOs "a key feature" in Ireland with explicit frameworks while the CP169 page says commencement has not happened: **the two statements conflict and were not resolved.** Not verified: the 1997 Act's text on subsidiaries, whether consolidated accounts are required, the Central Bank's financial-statement and returns rules.

**Solo versus consolidated.** Sources describe credit unions as supervised as single legal entities through the PRA Credit Unions Part and CREDS (S, snippets); the FCA page says nothing on group accounts. No credit-union-specific consolidated-accounts requirement was found in any of the three jurisdictions. IFRS for SMEs 9.2 requires a parent to present consolidated statements unless an exemption applies (P, read).

Researcher's inference: the unit of statutory reporting is always the legal entity, with a ledger per entity; GB credit union entities modelled with no controlled subsidiaries by default and a CUSO as a minority investment record, not a consolidation member; NI and Ireland may carry subsidiaries as a flag; consolidation is an optional layer defined by a group-structure record with control and ownership percentage. Purse, a credit union group across four jurisdictions, is therefore mostly a management-consolidation or reporting group, not a statutory one, to be confirmed with Purse's own advisers.

### 4.2 Management versus statutory consolidation sequencing
- **Business Central** (P: Learn "Set up company consolidation", updated 2026-06-02): a simple assisted setup for wholly owned units; partial ownership, excluded companies and per-account translation methods are "advanced"; reports include trial balance and G/L Consolidation Eliminations; a "Consolidation %" field covers partial ownership. It does not state a management-first or statutory-first sequence.
- **NetSuite OneWorld** (P: Oracle docs, snippets only): diagram the subsidiary hierarchy first with country, base currency and tax nexus per subsidiary; one elimination subsidiary as a child of each parent; some subsidiary fields lock after the first save. No phased-rollout guidance from Oracle; a consultant post (S, Kore1) warns hierarchy changes are hard to reverse.
- **Odoo** (P: multi-currency page): nothing on consolidation; S sources say multi-company selection gives combined P&L and balance sheet views with eliminations manual or via add-ons. The Odoo Consolidation documentation URL returned 404.
- **Xero** (S): no native consolidation; Fathom and Spotlight are the usual add-ons. Fathom's own blog says its consolidation is "designed for management insight and advisory use", not statutory or audit-ready; it uses average rates for P&L and closing rates for the balance sheet. Vendor entity limits: Fathom 300 entities single currency, 50 multi-currency (vendor claims).
- **Sequence.** No vendor states an explicit management-first rule and no implementation guide saying "do management consolidation first" was found; the sequence is inferred from product positioning (Xero add-ons are management-only by their vendors' description, while Business Central and NetSuite include eliminations and per-account translation). UK practitioner forum opinion (S): for management-only numbers, add entities together without parent-level adjustments.

Researcher's inference: the first release is management consolidation only (line-by-line combine, a mapped group chart of accounts, an elimination entity or journal set, period-end translation); statutory-grade features (non-controlling interest, goodwill, acquisition accounting, uniform policies under IFRS for SMEs 9.13-9.17) come in later flagged phases; hierarchy and base-currency settings are hard to change, so validate them at setup.

### 4.3 Intragroup foreign exchange and translation

IFRS for SMEs, 2025 third edition (P: ifrs.org HTML, read directly):
- 9.15 intragroup balances and transactions are eliminated in full; 9.16 same reporting date, or the latest statements adjusted for significant events; 9.17 uniform accounting policies.
- 30.7 and 30.9-30.10: a foreign currency transaction is recorded at the spot rate on its transaction date; monetary items are retranslated at the closing rate; differences go to profit or loss. 30.8 and 30.19: an average-rate approximation is allowed but "inappropriate" if rates fluctuate significantly.
- 30.12-30.13: a monetary item whose settlement is neither planned nor likely in the foreseeable future is part of the net investment; its differences are in profit or loss in the separate statements but in OCI (equity) in consolidated statements; trade receivables and payables are excluded.
- 30.17-30.18 (translation to presentation currency): assets and liabilities at the closing rate; income and expense at transaction-date rates (or an average per 30.19); all resulting differences in OCI, never reclassified.
- 30.22: an intragroup monetary balance cannot be eliminated without showing currency effects in the consolidated statements; the exchange difference stays in profit or loss unless it is net-investment (OCI); this holds for short-term and long-term items. 30.23: goodwill and fair value adjustments are treated as assets and liabilities of the foreign operation at the closing rate.
- **9.18B: the cumulative translation difference on a foreign subsidiary is not recycled to profit or loss on disposal, which differs from full IAS 21 (which does recycle). Flag this when choosing a standard.**
- 30.5A and Appendix 30A: if a currency is not exchangeable, estimate the spot rate and disclose it; this matters for thin-market currencies.

Full IFRS (IFRS 10, IAS 21 paragraph numbers) was **not re-read**; the researcher recalls IAS 21 paras 39, 45 and 32 and IFRS 10 B86 as consistent with the SME text, but cannot verify the numbers. The IFRS for SMEs text is the verified source. No IASB standard defines "management consolidation", so any approach is a policy choice; the Section 30 mechanics are the practical baseline, matched by Fathom (average for P&L, closing for balance sheet; vendor) and Business Central's per-account methods (average, closing, historical, composite, equity rate; P).

Researcher's inference: for a first release follow Section 30.17-30.22: translate the balance sheet at closing and P&L at the average rate, post differences to a translation reserve, do not silently eliminate cross-currency intragroup monetary balances (show the FX difference), add a per-balance "net investment / settlement not planned" flag routing the difference to OCI, fix the group presentation currency and each entity's functional currency in setup, defer hyperinflation and non-exchangeable handling but store a rate-source and exchangeability flag per currency pair now.

### 4.4 Department and branch (segment) accounting
- **Branch legal form.** A branch is the same legal person as its parent, with the parent liable; a subsidiary is a separate company with its own filings (S: Sprintlaw and Collyer Bristow guides; a more authoritative Collyer Bristow PDF not opened). An overseas company registers a UK establishment, not a new company, at Companies House (S). Branch accounts inside the parent's accounts: standard treatment but not confirmed from these sources. IFRS for SMEs 30.5 and 30.29 treat a branch as a foreign operation.
- **Business Central** (P: Learn finance dimensions, 2026-04-07): dimensions are tags (department, project, area), up to 2 global and 8 shortcut dimensions; per-account default dimensions with Value Posting options; "Code Mandatory" requires a dimension on that account, "Same Code" forces a fixed code; combinations can be blocked or limited; mandatory dimensions cannot be used on accounts touched by exchange-rate adjustment batch jobs.
- **Odoo** (P: analytic accounting, 18.0): each analytic plan has Default Applicability Optional, Mandatory or Unavailable; plan lines can override by document domain, account prefix, product category and company; Mandatory blocks confirmation until an analytic account is chosen. The documentation does not name the default and does not mention branches.
- **NetSuite** (S: Bill.com help, Oracle snippets): Department, Class and Location are native segments; global "Make ... Mandatory" preferences apply to all records and transactions (making a field required on specific forms is the narrower option); journals have separate preferences allowing non-balancing or empty classifications.
- **Xero** (S, no official page read): two active tracking categories per organisation; archiving is needed to add more; counts of options vary between sources; required versus optional status not found.
- **QuickBooks Online** (S): Class per line, Location per whole transaction; both need Plus or Advanced; a warn-me setting exists per third-party guides, not confirmed by Intuit.

Mandatory tagging per account: Business Central and Odoo give per-account or per-plan mandatory options; the others offer a global switch; the default in each product is optional or off (NetSuite, Xero and QuickBooks defaults not verified).

Researcher's inference: a branch is a tag or segment within one legal entity's ledger by default, with a separate ledger or entity only for a separately incorporated subsidiary; tagging optional at company level with a per-account "mandatory" rule off by default; an overridable default tag per account, partner or user; reports work with untagged lines (an "unassigned" bucket); keep tagging out of FX revaluation postings.

### 4.5 Exchange-rate sources, terms of use, and the rates tax authorities name

Central bank sources:
- **ECB** (P: euro reference rates page, disclaimer page): 30 currencies against the euro at about 16:00 CET on working days, not on TARGET closing days; CSV (zipped), XML, 90-day XML, SDMX-ML and RSS, plus a data portal bulk download. The page says "for information purposes only" and discourages their use for transactions. The legal notice allows free use crediting the ECB and stating any change; commercial reuse is not explicitly addressed. Naira, leone and other African currencies are not among them as far as seen (the full list was not checked).
- **Bank of England** (P: Database rates page and legal page): rates are "not official rates", no more authoritative than a commercial bank's in London. **The legal page says database reproduction falls under the Open Government Licence but that selected exchange rate data is excluded because LSEG owns the copyright, so reuse requests go to LSEG: a real risk for redistributing GBP rates.** A CSV endpoint exists, used by third-party tools (S); no automated-access rules stated but access can be restricted for "excessive use". Series codes, publication time and non-publication days were not confirmed.
- **Central Bank of Nigeria** (P: cbn.gov.ng rates page): publishes NFEM figures (rate, high, low, close, average, turnover, number of deals); the rate is a volume-weighted average that "stands as the official exchange rate for the day". The only export is "Export to Excel"; no terms of use or update frequency on the page; no documented API (third-party wrappers scrape or relay and carry their own terms: Apify, allratestoday; S).
- **Bank of Sierra Leone** (S mostly; bsl.gov.sl not opened): daily "Indicative Exchange Rates" as dated PDF sheets with buy, sell and mid rates against the SLE (a sheet "as at 31-Jul-26" was seen, with weighted-average mid-rates); no API seen; a vendor claims only a few weeks are kept online (unverified); terms of use could not be verified.

Rates named by each tax authority:
- **HMRC** (P: Corporate Finance Manual CFM61060): "Companies may use any reliable source of daily spot rates", plus monthly or yearly average rates, "These rates are acceptable for tax purposes"; the company must be consistent with its accounts; HMRC does not mandate a source. HMRC separately publishes monthly rates (S; licence terms not verified).
- **Revenue Ireland** (S, no primary read): an older note says VAT uses the latest selling rate recorded by the Central Bank of Ireland or the ECB when tax becomes due, or an alternative agreed with Revenue; Tax and Duty Manual 04-06-12 publishes Central Bank of Ireland annual average rates up to 2023; no corporation tax rate rule found.
- **Nigeria** (S: EY summary of the Nigeria Tax Act 2025): foreign-currency business expenses are deductible only if converted at the "official" naira rate (s.20(4)); the Act text defining "official" was not found by this researcher (the earlier tax researcher quoted s.20(4) from the Act, multi-entity digest 6.4, but also found no window named). Whether "official" means the CBN NFEM rate is an inference. A 2022 FIRS notice used the CBN I&E window rate as a one-off concession (S); FIRS has been replaced by the NRS.
- **Sierra Leone NRA:** no rate rule verified (only the Income Tax Act 2000 table of contents, S); a vendor says customs, the revenue authority and banks use the BSL rate (unverified). The earlier tax researcher found GST Act s.53 names the Bank of Sierra Leone mid-rate (multi-entity digest 6.5).

Researcher's inference: store the rate source, rate type (spot, average, closing) and fetch time with every rate, with the source configurable per tenant and jurisdiction; use the ECB (EUR base) as the main machine-readable source (the only one confirmed with documented bulk and API-style formats and a schedule); treat Bank of England and CBN data as manually or semi-manually imported (the BoE excludes some FX data from its open licence and the CBN offers only an Excel export) and do not auto-redistribute GBP or NGN rates to other tenants until the LSEG exclusion and terms are checked; treat the BSL as manual entry from the PDF until a feed and terms are confirmed; for tax, record the rate source the company used in its accounts (HMRC requires consistency) with a separate per-jurisdiction override for the return; carry the last published rate forward over weekends and holidays, flagged as carried forward.
