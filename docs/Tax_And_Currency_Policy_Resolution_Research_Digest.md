Researcher's inference (not a fact): lock a Company's jurisdiction and base currency at the first posting (or the first non-opening posting); before that allow changes; after that offer "create a new Company and migrate opening balances" as the only path.

### 1.2 Legal basis for the books currency, and ISO 4217 decimals

ISO 4217 minor units: GBP 2, EUR 2, NGN 2, SLE 2, LRD 2, USD 2, GNF 0, XOF 0. Source: the Wikipedia ISO 4217 table (S); the SIX official List One returned 404, so these are not confirmed against the registration authority. SLE is numeric 925 and replaced SLL on 1 July 2022 at 1000:1 (S: Wikipedia; Bank of Sierra Leone public notice, September 2022, via a SIX PDF).

- **Sierra Leone (best evidence).** The Bank of Sierra Leone (Amendment) Act 2024 (gazetted 13 March 2025, per a search summary of the parliament.gov.sl text; the Act itself could not be fetched, certificate error) replaces section 26 so that: the currency continues to be the Leone; prices of transactions in Sierra Leone shall be indicated in Leones; and the Leone is the currency for official purposes including accounting and financial reporting. It sets the prefix "NLe" for the new currency, with "Le" reserved for old-Leone references. Old notes ceased to be legal tender on 31 December 2023. The wording of any dollar-pricing exception is unverified. Wikipedia still shows "Le" (S).
- **Liberia.** Dual currency (LRD and USD) is the legal reality. A Central Bank of Liberia governor cites the 1999 CBL Act as making USD legal tender (S, undated). The Liberia Revenue Authority said in 2017 that taxpayers may pay in LRD or USD (S, FrontPageAfrica, 23 March 2017). Revenue Code s.6(c) translates foreign currency, or USD to LRD, at the Central Bank's published buying rate; s.6(a) covers USD payments at the market rate on the payment date (as quoted in that article; the primary Code text for this was not read by this researcher, but the earlier tax researcher read s.6(a) and found the books-in-LRD-or-USD rule, digest `Multi_Entity_Accounting_Research_Digest.md` 6.5). Methods of accounting ss.214-216 and the currency-fluctuation s.212 were not read. The CBL notice says "selling" for USD-stated taxes, so buying versus selling is a conflict.
- **Guinea.** The GNF is legal tender (S, Financial Afrik 2025). A third-party regulatory summary (S, regalert.today, February 2020) says Guinean francs are required for domestic invoicing and cross-border settlements must go through local banks. In 2018 the President ordered strict enforcement of the existing ban on foreign-currency invoicing (S, Guineenews). Guinea is an OHADA member (treaty in force 21 November 2000; P: ohada.com/etats-membres/guinee.html), so SYSCOHADA applies. The primary BCRG regulation and the tax code's currency clause were not found.
- **Cote d'Ivoire and UEMOA.** XOF (FCFA) is issued by the BCEAO, and Cote d'Ivoire is an OHADA member. The Ivorian tax authority's service note of 6 January 2016 requires financial statements under the OHADA accounting system (S/P: ohada.com). The Revised Uniform Act on Accounting Law (26 January 2017; in force 1 January 2018 for individual accounts) governs. The exact text on reporting currency could not be verified. (My knowledge, unverified: accounts in the CFA franc and tax declarations in FCFA.)
- **Nigeria.** The Naira is the only legal tender. The Central Bank of Nigeria's 2015 circulars call pricing goods in foreign currency illegal, citing CBN Act 2007 s.20(5) (P/S: Templars law note; BusinessDay); lawyers dispute that the Act bans foreign-currency pricing. The Corporate Affairs Commission has applied the Naira-only principle to share capital denomination (S: Aelex). The Nigeria Tax Administration Act 2025 has a section 39 "Currency of assessment and payment" (title confirmed in several PDFs; the earlier tax researcher read its text, digest 6.4). The Nigeria Tax Act 2025 is effective 1 January 2026 (S: EY, KPMG).
- **United Kingdom.** GBP. HMRC computes corporation tax profits in sterling, though accounts may use another presentation currency and a functional-currency computation applies (P: HMRC Corporate Finance Manual CFM64120 and the GIM4130 snippet). Companies Act 2006 s.394 and the local-currency election rules were not read.
- **Ireland.** EUR. Companies Act 2014 and Revenue rules not retrieved here (see the multi-entity digest 6.2).

Researcher's inference: ledger and tax currency come from the jurisdiction (SL=SLE, GN=GNF, CI=XOF, NG=NGN, UK=GBP, IE=EUR). Liberia is the real exception, since Liberian businesses legitimately keep books in either LRD or USD. Store amounts as integer minor units with a per-currency exponent (0 for GNF and XOF, 2 for the rest); never hard-code 2 decimals. Per the project's SLE-only onboarding decision only SLE needs seeding now, but the exponent table should be data-driven.

### 1.4 Cross-border payroll

Evidence is thin; the researcher could not read vendor documentation for Workday, ADP, Sage or SuccessFactors specifics.
- **Enterprise HCM norm (S/P, SAP product pages):** SuccessFactors Employee Central Payroll offers country-specific payroll for about 60 countries (53 by SAP, 7 by partners), with partners (Deel, Neeyamo) extending to 150+. This is the "separate legal entity per country plus a local payroll engine per country" model.
- **Shadow payroll (S: Remote, Applic8, HSP):** the employee stays on the home payroll and a parallel reporting-only calculation runs for the host country, usually triggered by tax residency (often 183 days). Different from split payroll, which divides actual pay. An enterprise mobility tool.
- **Employer of record (S: Deel):** the employer of record becomes the legal employer in the host country, signs the contract, runs payroll and withholds tax locally (Deel claims 100-150+ countries; counts vary by page).
- **Small-business payroll (S):** Xero payroll is per country and domestic; its Ideas forum shows no support for overseas addresses on AU payroll for foreign-resident employees, and a Xero reply said no near-term plans for other regions' payroll products. WhichPayroll (weak source) labels Xero UK "domestic only". QuickBooks and Sage: not verified.

Norm for small-business software (S, indirect): payroll is configured per legal entity in one country, with that country's tax rules; cross-border cases are pushed to employer-of-record or partner products.

Researcher's inference: payroll tax is computed under the jurisdiction of the paying Organisation, with the employee's tax jurisdiction defaulting to it; no shadow payroll or multi-jurisdiction employees; an employee working in another country needs a separate Organisation (legal entity) there, or is flagged out of scope and referred to an employer of record.

### 1.5 Should USD be a supported ledger currency in SL, LR, GN, NG, CI?

- **Liberia:** USD is legal tender alongside LRD; the dollar dominates (IMF working paper, about 90 percent of the money supply, dated 2009; S). Taxes may be paid in either currency (LRA 2017, S). USD books are legitimate; the primary rule on books currency was unverified by this researcher.
- **Sierra Leone:** the 2024 Amendment Act (search summary only) makes the Leone the official currency including accounting and financial reporting and says prices shall be indicated in Leones, which strongly suggests SLE-only books with USD as a transaction currency. Verify the full text.
- **Guinea:** the GNF is required for domestic invoicing and foreign-currency invoicing is banned (S, 2020 summary and 2018 news). Primary text not read.
- **Nigeria:** the Naira is the sole legal tender. The 2015 circular says foreign-currency pricing is illegal, though lawyers dispute its basis (S). Foreign-currency tax receipts were about 35.5 percent of 2025 tax revenue (S: Nairametrics/NBS), so USD payment occurs in practice. Oil and gas operators and certain agencies were exempted from the circulars (S). Whether NTAA s.39 permits non-Naira returns: see the multi-entity digest 6.4 (the text read there points to assessment and payment in the currency of the transaction).
- **Cote d'Ivoire:** search results did not establish pricing or legal-tender rules for XOF; could not verify. Treating XOF as the book currency (OHADA/UEMOA context) is an assumption.

Researcher's inference: treat USD as a transaction and foreign-balance currency (FX-valued, held in USD bank or cash accounts), not a ledger currency, for SL, GN, NG and CI; allow USD as an optional ledger currency only in Liberia, as a per-Company election locked at first posting. The Liberian books rule is the one point to confirm with a Liberian adviser, which the researcher notes is Femi's call as the tax professional.

## 2. VAT, GST and TVA behaviour (researcher A)

Coverage: strong and primary for the UK (HMRC Notice 700 and the instalments notice), Ireland (Revenue manuals and revenue.ie pages) and Guinea (a French "PROJET de Code General des Impots 2022", read directly but a draft); weak and secondary for Nigeria, Cote d'Ivoire, Liberia and Sierra Leone. The parliament.gov.sl GST Act PDF failed with a TLS certificate error and the NRA site had an expired certificate; neither was bypassed. Xero Central and developer pages returned empty bodies, so Xero statements are search-snippet quality. Liberia time-of-supply and invoice rules: not found.

### 2.1 Which date sets the rate
- **UK (P: gov.uk VAT Notice 700).** Basic tax point: goods, the date sent or taken away; services, the date performed (14.2.1). Actual tax point: if an invoice is issued or payment received before the basic tax point, whichever is first; an invoice issued within 14 days after the basic tax point makes the invoice date the tax point (14.2.2); "A tax point cannot be created simply by preparing an invoice." VAT is accounted for at the rate in force at the tax point (14.1). Rate-change option (30.7): normally the rate follows the tax point, but a supplier may instead use the rate at the basic tax point; on a rate rise the old rate may be used for goods removed or services performed before the change; on a fall the new rate may be used for supplies after the change even if invoiced or paid earlier, and any invoice at the old rate must then be corrected by credit note within 45 days of the change. HMRC's VAT time-of-supply manual VATTOS7210 says these rules do not alter the time of supply (P). Input VAT is reclaimed at the supplier's rate on the return for the period of the supplier's tax point (30.5, 10.5). VAT is accounted for in the period of the normal tax point even when the special rules are used; old and new rates are not split on the return (30.6).
- **Ireland (P: Revenue manual "Changes in rates of VAT", last reviewed August 2026, citing VATCA 2010 s.46).** Invoice basis, supply to a VAT-registered customer: the rate in force when the invoice is issued or should have been issued (1). Supply to an unregistered customer: the rate at the time of supply regardless of invoice date (1, 2). Moneys-received basis: the rate at the time of supply or of an advance payment (1, 4). VAT is due on the earlier of the invoice issue date or the date it should have been issued; the invoice is due within 15 days of the end of the month of supply (P: revenue.ie when-is-vat-due and what-is-vat-invoice); without an invoice, on the date of supply or of an advance payment. Invoices to registered customers issued on or after the change date show the new rate even for earlier supplies (2). Credit notes carry the rate in force when the original invoice was issued (registered customers) or at the time of supply (unregistered) (3).
- **Guinea (P, draft text).** Chargeable event (Art. 365): goods, delivery; imports, release for consumption; services, performance. Exigibility (Art. 366 III): goods at delivery; services when the price or an advance is collected, even before performance, so the invoice date is not the trigger; an option exists to tax services on debit entries (Art. 366 bis). Invoice issued no later than the chargeable event (Art. 383 V); it shows the invoice date and, where different, the delivery or performance date and the advance payment date (Art. 383 VI). Source: mbudget.gov.gn Code-General-des-Impots-2022-2.pdf.
- **Nigeria (S only).** Mondaq: VAT is charged when an invoice is issued (services) or a receipt issued (goods), or on payment, whichever is first. NTA s.153 (paraphrase via lawglobalhub): a VAT invoice must be issued at supply whether or not paid, with mandatory fields (supplier TIN, sequential number, supply date, buyer name, gross amount, VAT and rate). **PwC Tax Summaries (reviewed 29 May 2026) says Nigerian VAT returns are filed on a cash basis, which conflicts with "earlier of invoice or payment"; unresolved without the Act.** Statutory time-of-supply wording: unverified.
- **Cote d'Ivoire (S: ivoire-juriste blog citing CGI art. 361).** Goods: chargeable event and exigibility at delivery. Services and works: chargeable event on performance, exigibility on collection of advances or price. CGI text not read.
- **Sierra Leone and Liberia.** Sierra Leone GST Act 2009 s.19 is headed "Time of supply of goods" (heading only, from a snippet); the rule is unverified. Liberia unverified.
- **ERPs (stored dates).** Business Central (P-vendor): a separate VAT Date field beside posting and document date; default is posting or document date per General Ledger Setup, overridable per document, editable on posted VAT entries until the VAT return period closes; whether rates are looked up by VAT Date or posting date was not confirmed. NetSuite SuiteTax (S): a tax point date field defaults to the transaction date, is overridable, drives tax determination while the GL uses the transaction date. SAP Business One (S): a Tax Date field holds the date whose rules apply, defaulting from the document or posting date; tax codes can carry Valid From dates. Odoo (S): standard tax follows the invoice date (accounting date separately editable); cash-basis taxes are recorded at payment; an OCA module exists for Dutch invoice-basis dating; no official documentation of a separate tax point date found. Sage Accounting (P-vendor): the rate follows the transaction (invoice) date ("Transactions dated 1 March or later use 23%", Irish rate-change page); under the cash accounting scheme the rate depends on the invoice date, not the payment date, which conflicts with UK law for cash-accounting users who account on payment. QuickBooks UK (P-vendor): rate edits apply to new transactions only; backdated entries use the new rate; no separate tax point date found. Xero: not verified.

Researcher's inference: store the invoice date and a separate "tax point date" on each posting with a documented derivation per jurisdiction; key the rate lookup off the tax point date and record the date used and the reason on the line; for Ireland business-to-business invoice basis the tax point is the invoice date or the date it was due, for business-to-consumer the supply date; keep the user-overridable UK 30.7 options off the default path; Guinea needs the payment date as the trigger for services.

### 2.2 A supply that straddles a rate change
- **UK (P: Notice 700 30.8, 30.9, 30.12; gov.uk VAT instalments, deposits and credit sales).** Services spanning a change where the work can be apportioned on measurable work or normal costing: the old rate may be applied to pre-change work and the new rate to later work, with a credit note if this reduces VAT already invoiced. Continuous supplies are normally charged at the rate at each tax point (30.9.1); a forward invoice covering up to 12 months becomes invalid for post-change payments, so fresh invoices are needed; alternatively account at the old rate for pre-change parts and the new rate for post-change parts, based on value actually supplied (30.9.2). Advance payments are normally taxed at the rate when paid or invoiced; if the rate changes before the supply, the rate at supply may be used with a credit note for any overcharge (30.12). Existing contracts: the price moves by the tax change unless the contract says otherwise (30.15).
- **Ireland (P).** Continuous utility supplies: the rate in force when the bill issues, provided bills are at least quarterly; otherwise the rate at time of supply (rate-change manual 6). Fixed-interval payments: "Fixed interval payments that become due before the date of a VAT rate change and which are in respect of a period of time spanning that date may be treated, for the purposes of transition only, as taxable at the old rates if invoiced before the date of the change" (example: quarterly advance rentals; 5), the closest Irish analogue to a school term billed in advance. Contracts: price adjusted by the tax change unless the parties agree otherwise (5). Advance payments: the rate at the invoice for that payment (4). **Worked example for the 1 July 2026 change** (P: Revenue manual "VAT treatment of Guest and Holiday Accommodation"): the 9% second reduced rate applies to restaurant and catering from 1 July 2026 while accommodation stays at 13.5%; for a package booked in June for July with an advance payment in June, "The VAT due on this payment will all be at the reduced rate"; the July final payment is split (accommodation 13.5%, food 9%); a single-price package is apportioned "on a fair and reasonable basis" by reference to standalone selling prices (a 150 stay with a 20 breakfast: 130 at 13.5% VAT 15.46, 20 at 9% VAT 1.65). Announced in eBrief 086/26 (28 April 2026). Apportionment by days is not mandated anywhere read; the Irish example is invoice-timing-driven.
- **Guinea (P, draft).** Services are taxed on collection, so a term paid in advance is taxed at collection; rate changes are not addressed in the text read. Education by approved establishments is exempt (Art. 362 I.18), which matters for school fees there.
- **Other jurisdictions:** unverified (Nigeria, Cote d'Ivoire, Liberia, Sierra Leone). Liberia's rate is in flux: GST 12% to 13% from 1 May 2026 with a planned VAT replacement on 1 January 2027 (S: vatupdate.com); **sources disagree on the 2027 VAT rate (PwC 15%, other sources 18%).**
- **ERPs.** Business Central's VAT Rate Change tool converts master data, journals and open orders to new posting groups but does not convert orders with posted shipments (they stay at the old rate), documents with posted prepayment invoices, credit memos or return orders, which need manual handling. Sage: re-edit and re-save post-change transactions that picked up the old rate (unallocating payments first), update or pause recurring invoices, credit notes carry an "original invoice date" so they use the original rate. QuickBooks: manual edit of existing transactions and recurring templates. No ERP read does day-apportionment automatically; split-rate lines are a manual two-line entry.

Researcher's inference: no automatic day-apportionment; a document line has one tax point date and one rate; provide a "split across change date" helper producing two lines (or an invoice plus a credit note, as UK 30.7 and 30.8 require); do not auto-pick between old and new rates, since the authorities offer options, not mandates.

### 2.3 VAT registration as a business fact
- **Thresholds (current).** UK (P): 90,000 pounds to register; deregistration 88,000 pounds (gov.uk search result); voluntary registration allowed below. Ireland (P): services only 42,500; goods 85,000; mixed supplies 85,000 where 90% or more is goods; voluntary registration allowed. Guinea (P, draft): VAT "franchise" below GNF 1,000,000,000 turnover in the previous year (Art. 359); opt-in available above GNF 500,000,000 turnover or investment (Art. 360). Sierra Leone (S): SLE 500,000 (PKF snapshot, 2025); other sources say SLE 350,000 and the old Le 200,000,000: **sources disagree.** Nigeria (S): small business exempt from VAT registration, charging and filing if turnover is 100m naira or less and fixed assets 250m or less, professional services excluded (Forvis Mazars); other blogs cite 25m or 50m: **sources disagree**, unresolved without the Act. Cote d'Ivoire (S): VAT charged only by businesses under the real regime with annual turnover including tax above FCFA 200 million; other sources mention 5m, 50m and 100m figures for other regimes and options: unresolved. Liberia (S): PwC lists US$20,000 for VAT from 2027; other sources stale or conflicting: unresolved.
- **Effect on invoicing.** UK (P): "Only a VAT-registered person can issue a VAT invoice" (19.5.1); pre-registration VAT on purchases is not input tax (11.1), subject to the conditions in 11. Ireland (P): an unregistered trader should not issue an invoice showing VAT and is liable for VAT shown plus possible penalties (revenue.ie invoices-issued-by-unregistered-persons); with exempt and taxable activities, only VAT on the taxable part is reclaimable. Guinea (P, draft): a franchise business must still issue invoices (Art. 359 III), must not show VAT, must state "TVA non applicable - article 359 du CGI", may not deduct VAT on purchases; anyone who wrongly shows VAT on an invoice owes it (Art. 373 bis V). Nigeria, Sierra Leone, Liberia, Cote d'Ivoire: unverified.
- **Irrecoverable VAT into cost.** IAS 2's cost of purchase includes taxes not later recoverable (S summaries only; ifrs.org text not reached). The authorities' pages say unregistered persons cannot reclaim but none says "put it in cost": that is an accounting rule, not a VAT rule.
- **ERPs.** QuickBooks UK (S, snippet): once VAT is set up it cannot be turned off; workaround is to default every code to "No VAT". Xero (snippet only): moving off a VAT scheme sets default tax rates to "No VAT"; a correcting journal if purchases were entered with VAT while unregistered. Business Central: driven by VAT business and product posting groups. Odoo: no first-class "not VAT registered" flag in core found. Sage: forum guidance only.

Researcher's inference: model registration as a dated fact on the Organisation (status, VAT number, effective-from); unregistered means the tax code is forced to "no VAT" on sales, purchase VAT is added to the cost line instead of an input VAT account, and the invoice shows no VAT; on registration, new postings change from that date and history is not rewritten; the threshold is a monitoring alert, not an automatic switch, since registration is a legal act.

### 2.4 Exports, zero-rating, reverse charge, exempt
- **UK (P).** Zero-rated: no VAT payable, but the supplier is still treated as making taxable supplies and generally recovers input tax (3.5). Exempt: no VAT, input tax "not normally" recoverable (3.6). Exports zero-rated if conditions are met (5.5; evidence is in Notice 703, not read). B2B services from abroad: the customer accounts under the reverse charge (5.4). Invoice-based tax points do not apply to zero-rated and exempt supplies (15.10).
- **Ireland (P).** Export evidence includes the IE599 export notification and bill of lading (revenue.ie vat-and-exports). Reverse charge invoices carry the customer's VAT number and a notation and no VAT amount. Zero-rated and exempt are distinguished by the right to deduct input VAT (Notes for Guidance; the extract was cut off).
- **Guinea (P, draft).** 0% for direct exports and related services, international transport and some ship and aircraft operations (Art. 373 II); registration required to make zero-rated supplies (Art. 373 quater); many exemptions (Art. 362) including approved education and medical care; reverse charge when a non-established supplier sells to a Guinea-registered customer (Art. 373 bis II).
- **Nigeria (S: Mondaq, Forvis, PwC).** Zero-rated: exported goods (excluding oil and gas), exported services and incorporeal property, with input VAT recoverable and refundable. Exempt: includes oil and gas exports, input VAT not recoverable. Basic food, medical and education items were moved to zero-rated and diplomatic purchases to exempt (Forvis). PwC says exported services are zero-rated.
- **Cote d'Ivoire (S): sources disagree** on whether exports are "exempt" (arts. 356 and 357, with an exporter refund under art. 382) or 0% with deductible input VAT; CGI not read. **Sierra Leone (S: PKF):** standard 15%, zero-rated exports, exempt (water excluding bottled, medical supplies), out of scope (going-concern sales, mergers). **Liberia (S):** exports 0%.
- **ERPs.** The per-line tax code is the normal model, defaulted from the item or product and from the customer's type or tax zone. Odoo: fiscal positions remap product taxes (for example export to a 0% tax), selected at document level and detected automatically by country and VAT number (whether individual lines can still be overridden is not stated; a forum post says tax mappings were removed from fiscal positions from 18.3, conflicting with the 19.0 docs). Business Central: VAT business group (customer side) combined with VAT product group (item side) gives the VAT posting setup; calculation types Normal, Reverse Charge, Full VAT, Sales Tax; per-document VAT amount edits allowed within a "Max. VAT Difference Allowed" tolerance; "Certificate of Supply" tracking for EU shipments (if not received, a new invoice including VAT must be sent). QuickBooks UK: codes S, R, Z, No VAT (exempt), No VAT (out of scope), reverse-charge and EU B2B codes mapping to return boxes; "No VAT" does not appear in the VAT100 for an export, so community guidance steers exports to Z (S). Sage UK: T0, T1, T2 (exempt), T9 (outside scope); no custom rates, a different rate means picking Standard and overriding the VAT value (forum, S). Per-transaction override with audit: Business Central keeps a posted VAT Entry with its own VAT Date and edit rules tied to the return period, and a "VAT Difference" field records manual amount changes; SuiteTax has an explicit tax-point-date override; Odoo logs lock-date exceptions in the company chatter. **No ERP with a general "reason code + approver" audit on tax-code override was found (unverified as a gap).**

Researcher's inference: keep exempt, zero-rated, outside-scope, reverse-charge and standard or reduced as separate tax categories (not rates), because input-VAT recovery differs between zero-rated and exempt; default the category from the item and customer; allow a per-line override that requires a reason and records who changed it; treat the Cote d'Ivoire export classification as an open decision.

### 2.5 The local tax word and invoice labels
- **UK (P).** The VAT invoice must show sequential number, time of supply, supplier name, address and VAT number, customer name and address, description, quantity, rate and net amount per line, total excluding VAT, the VAT total in sterling, and unit price (16.3.1); the words "VAT invoice" are not required by the excerpt read; simplified invoices for 250 pounds or less (16.6).
- **Ireland (P: revenue.ie).** Date, sequential number, names and addresses, supplier VAT number, VAT-exclusive prices, breakdown by rate, total VAT; special notations: reverse charge (customer's VAT number plus notation), margin scheme ("Margin scheme - second-hand goods"), foreign-currency invoices must also show euro equivalents.
- **Guinea (P, draft).** French "TVA"; mandatory mentions include the tax identifiers, sequential number, invoice date, delivery date and advance date if different, unit price excluding tax and rate per line or the Code reference for exemption, total VAT; franchise invoices say "TVA non applicable - article 359 du CGI"; 50% withholding invoices say "Retenue de 50% de la TVA; article 373 ter du CGI".
- **Nigeria (S).** The term is "VAT"; NTA s.153 fields as in 2.1; e-invoicing and fiscalisation are phased in from 2026 (Forvis). **Cote d'Ivoire (S).** "TVA"; the invoice must show price excluding tax, rate, VAT and an "exigibility" mention (search summary); a normalised electronic invoice is reportedly required from 1 December 2025 (unverified). **Sierra Leone and Liberia.** The tax is called GST; invoice content unverified.
- **ERPs.** Business Central supports VAT clauses per VAT posting setup and document type with translations by customer language, printing exemption or non-standard-rate text on documents. Xero: newer templates default the title to "Tax Invoice"; editing the title is an open product-idea request (forum, S); UAE users report the layout does not meet local requirements. No documentation of country label localisation found for QuickBooks, Sage, Odoo, SAP or NetSuite (unverified).

Researcher's inference: store the jurisdiction's tax word (VAT, GST, TVA) as localisation data, not a hard-coded label; templates compose mandatory fields per jurisdiction; support per-tax-code legal text as Business Central's VAT clause does.

### 2.6 A wrong rate was posted: correction
- **UK (P: VAT Notice 700/45).** Method 1 (adjust on the current return): net errors up to 10,000 pounds, or 10,000 to 50,000 and not more than 1% of box 6 for the period the error is found. Method 2 (notify HMRC): over the 1% limit, over 50,000, or any deliberate error. Time limit 4 years; tax point errors may be corrected if the VAT was declared on the immediately preceding or following return. Records: date found, period of error, related documents, output or input tax. A wrong rate on invoices issued: if VAT was overstated, you still account for the higher amount unless you correct it with the customer by credit note; if understated, account for the correct amount whether or not you issue a supplementary invoice. Wrong VAT on invoices received: ask for a replacement invoice; account for the VAT shown until you have it. The notice does not treat "wrong rate" as a separate topic. **gov.uk's return-correction page words the 1% test differently (1% of box 6 versus "1% of total value of sales" on another gov.uk page): sources disagree.**
- **Ireland (P: Code of Practice for Revenue Compliance Interventions).** Bi-monthly, quarterly or half-yearly remitters self-correcting a net underpayment of less than 6,000 (the currency symbol was lost in extraction; euro presumably) may include it as an adjustment on the next corresponding VAT return following the one in which the error was made; no notification and no interest (2.2.1). No penalty where the aggregate default is under 6,000 and not deliberate (s.1077F TCA 1997 per the same document). Above that, or later, a written qualifying disclosure is available (penalty tables not extracted and may be dated). The edition date of the Code was not visible; the 6,000 threshold may have changed: verify on revenue.ie.
- **Guinea (P, draft).** Omitted deductible VAT can be corrected on later returns filed by 31 December of the year following the omission if payment of the invoices is proved (Art. 375 V); an invoice without VAT when the supply was taxable means the supplier owes VAT calculated inside the invoiced amount (Art. 373 bis VI). Nigeria, Sierra Leone, Liberia, Cote d'Ivoire: unverified.
- **ERPs.** Business Central: edit the VAT Date on posted VAT entries until the return period closes; "Control VAT Period" blocks posting in closed periods; "Allow VAT Date From/To" restricts date ranges; posted VAT amount corrections via a dedicated correction VAT product posting group. Odoo (S, forum and Odoo 19 tax return docs): a locked invoice cannot be adjusted, the fix is a credit note and re-invoice in an open period; a tax-return lock date moves corrections to the next open period; a hard lock cannot be reversed. Sage (P-vendor): edit and re-save the invoice (unallocate payments first); credit notes carry the original invoice date. QuickBooks UK: edit existing transactions manually; a "Make VAT adjustments" feature exists (page not read). Xero (snippet only): paid transactions can be edited several ways or by manual journal; unpaid transactions allow a direct VAT amount adjustment. No ERP documentation of a "find all postings with this rate and date" tool was found, except Business Central's VAT Rate Change log of converted entries and test-conversion mode; reports filtered by tax code and period are the usual route (not specifically documented in what was read).

Researcher's inference: corrections are new dated postings (a credit note and re-invoice, or an adjustment entry), never edits to a posting in a filed or locked period; record on each correction the reason, original document and period; give a "tax code or rate used on lines between dates X and Y" report with a threshold flag per jurisdiction (UK 10,000 pounds / 1% of box 6 / 50,000 pounds; Ireland 6,000), with thresholds as configuration data.

### 3.6 Corporate tax rate change inside an accounting period
- **United Kingdom (P).** CTA 2010 s3: "Corporation tax is charged at the rate set by Parliament for the financial year as the main rate." CTA 2010 s1172: "Any apportionment to different periods which falls to be made under the Corporation Tax Acts is to be made on a time basis... subject to any provision to the contrary." A straddling period (ATT, S, att.org.uk, reported as HMRC's confirmed view) is split into two notional periods on the rate-change date and total profits including chargeable gains are time-apportioned (example: 90/365 and 275/365 at 1 April 2023). The small-profits limits of 50,000 and 250,000 pounds are reduced pro rata for the shorter notional period (ATT, S). The section number the researcher first expected (s8) was wrong; only s3 and s1172 were verified.
- **Ireland (P).** TCA 1997 s26(3): "Corporation tax for any financial year shall be charged on profits arising in that year; but assessments to corporation tax shall be made on a company by reference to accounting periods, and the amount chargeable... shall where necessary be apportioned between the financial years in which the accounting period falls." The Revenue Note for Guidance (S, charteredaccountants.ie mirror) says "This apportionment of profits is made on a time basis by virtue of section 4(6)." The Irish Statute Book returned 403; Revenue's own manual text not found.
- **Nigeria (S).** The transition rule is disputed. The Minister's General Transition Guidelines (cited in a Joe Adinma article, S) say income of an accounting period ending before 1 January 2026 stays under the old, repealed law regardless of filing date; the same article reports NRS guidance of February 2026 took a filing-based approach. No source explains how a period straddling 1 January 2026 is split. Could not verify.
- **Sierra Leone, Liberia, Guinea, Cote d'Ivoire:** no statute text or guidance on straddling periods found. Could not verify any.
- **Tax software.** Dynamics AX (P) applies From/To-dated values by transaction date; Oracle and SAP use dated rate rows per transaction (P/S). No vendor documentation of corporate-income-tax apportionment inside the accounting engine was found; it is generally a tax-computation or return product's job.

Researcher's inference: store the rate by financial year or effective window; split a straddling period into notional sub-periods on the rate-change date and time-apportion taxable profit by days with pro-rated thresholds; apply this as the default only for the UK and Ireland, where it is statutory; for the other five jurisdictions (including Nigeria, where authorities conflict) flag the period "straddle - manual determination" rather than assuming.

## 4. Multi-entity open decisions (researcher D)

### 4.1 Credit union group reporting and subsidiaries (UK, Northern Ireland, Ireland)

**Great Britain** (P: PRA letter of 26 March 2025, read in full; PRA PS5/26, February 2026).

- A CUSO is a minority investment below subsidiary control. How GB credit unions account for it (equity method, cost) could not be verified.

**Northern Ireland** (P: the same PRA letter and PS5/26). Both state there is no express prohibition on forming a subsidiary in NI legislation and that the s.26 prohibition does not apply to NI credit unions. Regulators: PRA and FCA; the FCA runs registration; the Department for the Economy covers policy (S, snippet only). Legislation: Credit Unions (NI) Order 1985 plus the NI 2016 Act (S). Could not verify whether NI credit unions actually have subsidiaries or prepare group accounts; only the absence of a legal bar was found.

**Republic of Ireland** (P: Central Bank CP169 page; S: search snippets). The Credit Union (Amendment) Act 2023, s.24, will allow investment in CUSOs once commenced ("Phase 4"). CP169, a consultation on a CUSO framework, was published 12 June 2026 with responses due 12 August 2026; not final. The CP169 page does not say whether a credit union may own or control a CUSO or how it is accounted for; the 665 KB PDF was not read. The PRA letter calls CUSOs "a key feature" in Ireland with explicit frameworks while the CP169 page says commencement has not happened: **the two statements conflict and were not resolved.** Not verified: the 1997 Act's text on subsidiaries, whether consolidated accounts are required, the Central Bank's financial-statement and returns rules.

**Solo versus consolidated.** Sources describe credit unions as supervised as single legal entities through the PRA Credit Unions Part and CREDS (S, snippets); the FCA page says nothing on group accounts. No credit-union-specific consolidated-accounts requirement was found in any of the three jurisdictions. IFRS for SMEs 9.2 requires a parent to present consolidated statements unless an exemption applies (P, read).

Researcher's inference: the unit of statutory reporting is always the legal entity, with a ledger per entity; GB credit union entities modelled with no controlled subsidiaries by default and a CUSO as a minority investment record, not a consolidation member; NI and Ireland may carry subsidiaries as a flag; consolidation is an optional layer defined by a group-structure record with control and ownership percentage. Purse, a credit union group across four jurisdictions, is therefore mostly a management-consolidation or reporting group, not a statutory one, to be confirmed with Purse's own advisers.

- **Business Central** (P: Learn "Set up company consolidation", updated 2026-06-02): a simple assisted setup for wholly owned units; partial ownership, excluded companies and per-account translation methods are "advanced"; reports include trial balance and G/L Consolidation Eliminations; a "Consolidation %" field covers partial ownership. No phased-rollout guidance from Oracle; a consultant post (S, Kore1) warns hierarchy changes are hard to reverse.
- **Odoo** (P: multi-currency page): nothing on consolidation; S sources say multi-company selection gives combined P&L and balance sheet views with eliminations manual or via add-ons. The Odoo Consolidation documentation URL returned 404.
- **Xero** (S): no native consolidation; Fathom and Spotlight are the usual add-ons. Fathom's own blog says its consolidation is "designed for management insight and advisory use", not statutory or audit-ready; it uses average rates for P&L and closing rates for the balance sheet. Vendor entity limits: Fathom 300 entities single currency, 50 multi-currency (vendor claims).

Researcher's inference: the first release is management consolidation only (line-by-line combine, a mapped group chart of accounts, an elimination entity or journal set, period-end translation); statutory-grade features (non-controlling interest, goodwill, acquisition accounting, uniform policies under IFRS for SMEs 9.13-9.17) come in later flagged phases; hierarchy and base-currency settings are hard to change, so validate them at setup.

### 4.3 Intragroup foreign exchange and translation

IFRS for SMEs, 2025 third edition (P: ifrs.org HTML, read directly):
- 9.15 intragroup balances and transactions are eliminated in full; 9.16 same reporting date, or the latest statements adjusted for significant events; 9.17 uniform accounting policies.
- 30.7 and 30.9-30.10: a foreign currency transaction is recorded at the spot rate on its transaction date; monetary items are retranslated at the closing rate; differences go to profit or loss. 30.8 and 30.19: an average-rate approximation is allowed but "inappropriate" if rates fluctuate significantly.
- 30.12-30.13: a monetary item whose settlement is neither planned nor likely in the foreseeable future is part of the net investment; its differences are in profit or loss in the separate statements but in OCI (equity) in consolidated statements; trade receivables and payables are excluded.
- 30.17-30.18 (translation to presentation currency): assets and liabilities at the closing rate; income and expense at transaction-date rates (or an average per 30.19); all resulting differences in OCI, never reclassified.
- 30.22: an intragroup monetary balance cannot be eliminated without showing currency effects in the consolidated statements; the exchange difference stays in profit or loss unless it is net-investment (OCI); this holds for short-term and long-term items. 30.23: goodwill and fair value adjustments are treated as assets and liabilities of the foreign operation at the closing rate.
- **9.18B: the cumulative translation difference on a foreign subsidiary is not recycled to profit or loss on disposal, which differs from full IAS 21 (which does recycle). Flag this when choosing a standard.**
- 30.5A and Appendix 30A: if a currency is not exchangeable, estimate the spot rate and disclose it; this matters for thin-market currencies.

Full IFRS (IFRS 10, IAS 21 paragraph numbers) was **not re-read**; the researcher recalls IAS 21 paras 39, 45 and 32 and IFRS 10 B86 as consistent with the SME text, but cannot verify the numbers. The IFRS for SMEs text is the verified source. No IASB standard defines "management consolidation", so any approach is a policy choice; the Section 30 mechanics are the practical baseline, matched by Fathom (average for P&L, closing for balance sheet; vendor) and Business Central's per-account methods (average, closing, historical, composite, equity rate; P).

Researcher's inference: for a first release follow Section 30.17-30.22: translate the balance sheet at closing and P&L at the average rate, post differences to a translation reserve, do not silently eliminate cross-currency intragroup monetary balances (show the FX difference), add a per-balance "net investment / settlement not planned" flag routing the difference to OCI, fix the group presentation currency and each entity's functional currency in setup, defer hyperinflation and non-exchangeable handling but store a rate-source and exchangeability flag per currency pair now.

### 4.4 Department and branch (segment) accounting
- **Branch legal form.** A branch is the same legal person as its parent, with the parent liable; a subsidiary is a separate company with its own filings (S: Sprintlaw and Collyer Bristow guides; a more authoritative Collyer Bristow PDF not opened). An overseas company registers a UK establishment, not a new company, at Companies House (S). Branch accounts inside the parent's accounts: standard treatment but not confirmed from these sources. IFRS for SMEs 30.5 and 30.29 treat a branch as a foreign operation.
- **Business Central** (P: Learn finance dimensions, 2026-04-07): dimensions are tags (department, project, area), up to 2 global and 8 shortcut dimensions; per-account default dimensions with Value Posting options; "Code Mandatory" requires a dimension on that account, "Same Code" forces a fixed code; combinations can be blocked or limited; mandatory dimensions cannot be used on accounts touched by exchange-rate adjustment batch jobs.
- **Odoo** (P: analytic accounting, 18.0): each analytic plan has Default Applicability Optional, Mandatory or Unavailable; plan lines can override by document domain, account prefix, product category and company; Mandatory blocks confirmation until an analytic account is chosen. The documentation does not name the default and does not mention branches.
- **NetSuite** (S: Bill.com help, Oracle snippets): Department, Class and Location are native segments; global "Make ... Mandatory" preferences apply to all records and transactions (making a field required on specific forms is the narrower option); journals have separate preferences allowing non-balancing or empty classifications.
- **Xero** (S, no official page read): two active tracking categories per organisation; archiving is needed to add more; counts of options vary between sources; required versus optional status not found.
- **QuickBooks Online** (S): Class per line, Location per whole transaction; both need Plus or Advanced; a warn-me setting exists per third-party guides, not confirmed by Intuit.

Mandatory tagging per account: Business Central and Odoo give per-account or per-plan mandatory options; the others offer a global switch; the default in each product is optional or off (NetSuite, Xero and QuickBooks defaults not verified).

Researcher's inference: a branch is a tag or segment within one legal entity's ledger by default, with a separate ledger or entity only for a separately incorporated subsidiary; tagging optional at company level with a per-account "mandatory" rule off by default; an overridable default tag per account, partner or user; reports work with untagged lines (an "unassigned" bucket); keep tagging out of FX revaluation postings.

### 4.5 Exchange-rate sources, terms of use, and the rates tax authorities name

Central bank sources:
- **ECB** (P: euro reference rates page, disclaimer page): 30 currencies against the euro at about 16:00 CET on working days, not on TARGET closing days; CSV (zipped), XML, 90-day XML, SDMX-ML and RSS, plus a data portal bulk download. The page says "for information purposes only" and discourages their use for transactions. The legal notice allows free use crediting the ECB and stating any change; commercial reuse is not explicitly addressed. Naira, leone and other African currencies are not among them as far as seen (the full list was not checked).
- **Bank of England** (P: Database rates page and legal page): rates are "not official rates", no more authoritative than a commercial bank's in London. **The legal page says database reproduction falls under the Open Government Licence but that selected exchange rate data is excluded because LSEG owns the copyright, so reuse requests go to LSEG: a real risk for redistributing GBP rates.** A CSV endpoint exists, used by third-party tools (S); no automated-access rules stated but access can be restricted for "excessive use". Series codes, publication time and non-publication days were not confirmed.
- **Central Bank of Nigeria** (P: cbn.gov.ng rates page): publishes NFEM figures (rate, high, low, close, average, turnover, number of deals); the rate is a volume-weighted average that "stands as the official exchange rate for the day". The only export is "Export to Excel"; no terms of use or update frequency on the page; no documented API (third-party wrappers scrape or relay and carry their own terms: Apify, allratestoday; S).
- **Bank of Sierra Leone** (S mostly; bsl.gov.sl not opened): daily "Indicative Exchange Rates" as dated PDF sheets with buy, sell and mid rates against the SLE (a sheet "as at 31-Jul-26" was seen, with weighted-average mid-rates); no API seen; a vendor claims only a few weeks are kept online (unverified); terms of use could not be verified.

Rates named by each tax authority:
- **HMRC** (P: Corporate Finance Manual CFM61060): "Companies may use any reliable source of daily spot rates", plus monthly or yearly average rates, "These rates are acceptable for tax purposes"; the company must be consistent with its accounts; HMRC does not mandate a source. HMRC separately publishes monthly rates (S; licence terms not verified).
- **Revenue Ireland** (S, no primary read): an older note says VAT uses the latest selling rate recorded by the Central Bank of Ireland or the ECB when tax becomes due, or an alternative agreed with Revenue; Tax and Duty Manual 04-06-12 publishes Central Bank of Ireland annual average rates up to 2023; no corporation tax rate rule found.
- **Nigeria** (S: EY summary of the Nigeria Tax Act 2025): foreign-currency business expenses are deductible only if converted at the "official" naira rate (s.20(4)); the Act text defining "official" was not found by this researcher (the earlier tax researcher quoted s.20(4) from the Act, multi-entity digest 6.4, but also found no window named). Whether "official" means the CBN NFEM rate is an inference. A 2022 FIRS notice used the CBN I&E window rate as a one-off concession (S); FIRS has been replaced by the NRS.
- **Sierra Leone NRA:** no rate rule verified (only the Income Tax Act 2000 table of contents, S); a vendor says customs, the revenue authority and banks use the BSL rate (unverified). The earlier tax researcher found GST Act s.53 names the Bank of Sierra Leone mid-rate (multi-entity digest 6.5).

Researcher's inference: store the rate source, rate type (spot, average, closing) and fetch time with every rate, with the source configurable per tenant and jurisdiction; use the ECB (EUR base) as the main machine-readable source (the only one confirmed with documented bulk and API-style formats and a schedule); treat Bank of England and CBN data as manually or semi-manually imported (the BoE excludes some FX data from its open licence and the CBN offers only an Excel export) and do not auto-redistribute GBP or NGN rates to other tenants until the LSEG exclusion and terms are checked; treat the BSL as manual entry from the PDF until a feed and terms are confirmed; for tax, record the rate source the company used in its accounts (HMRC requires consistency) with a separate per-jurisdiction override for the return; carry the last published rate forward over weekends and holidays, flagged as carried forward.

### 5.3 Nigeria

**Tax-year definition and the 2026 regime change.** Year of assessment (Nigeria Tax Act 2025, section 202 definitions, VERIFIED in the TheNigeriaLawyer copy): "government tax year being 1st of January to 31 of December of any year." Companies: the accounting year can differ; returns are due six months after the accounting year end (Nigeria Tax Administration Act s.11(5), VERIFIED, gazette No. 117 Vol. 112, 26 June 2025). Both Acts were assented on 26 June 2025 and took effect on 1 January 2026 (the commencement bracket was blank in the Act text read; the date is SECONDARY: judy.legal, allAfrica). The Nigeria Revenue Service replaced FIRS on 1 January 2026 (SECONDARY). What the repealed law used as the year, and whether it moved from a preceding-year to a current-year basis: could not verify (PwC, SECONDARY, still describes a preceding-year basis). **Transition dispute (SECONDARY, Joe Adinma):** NRS guidance of February 2026 applies the new Acts to any company income tax return falling due in 2026 whatever period it covers; the Minister of Finance's General Transition Guidelines of 18 June 2026 (para 10.1.2(1)) say tax follows the basis period, so accounting periods ending before 1 January 2026 stay under the repealed law; the NRS Rev360 portal (live end April 2026) enforces its own reading; the Guidelines themselves were not read. **Which text:** the text read is the FIRS gazette-version copy; BusinessDay reports the signed "Final Approved Copy" differs (below); the signed copy was not obtained.

**Tax types and authority.** VAT (NRS federal; monthly return). PAYE (employer deducts; the State Internal Revenue Service for resident individuals, NRS for corporate and federal matters). Personal Income Tax (individuals and sole traders; State tax authority, NTAA s.3(2) and the First Schedule). Companies Income Tax (NRS). Development Levy (companies other than small and non-resident; NRS). Minimum effective tax rate top-up 15% (large companies and MNE constituents). Withholding Tax (NRS or State). Capital gains (companies in profits; individuals in total income). Stamp duties (NRS). Pension (Pension Reform Act 2014; PenCom and pension fund administrators). National Housing Fund (Federal Mortgage Bank). NSITF Employee Compensation (employer). Industrial Training Fund (employer). The authority split is VERIFIED in NTAA s.3.

**VAT.** 7.5% on taxable supplies (VERIFIED, NTA s.148; unchanged since 1 February 2020, SECONDARY PwC, so 2024, 2025 and 2026 are all 7.5%). Monthly return due the 21st of the following month (VERIFIED, NTAA s.22); an extension to file does not extend time to pay. Small business exemption: gross turnover of 50 million naira or less a year with fixed assets under 250 million, excluding professional services; exempt from registration, charging and returns; may opt out by written notice (VERIFIED in the gazette NTAA text; the threshold is disputed, below). Exempt supplies (s.186) include oil and gas exports, baby products and shared road passenger transport; zero-rated per PwC (SECONDARY): basic food, medical and education items. VAT withheld by agents remitted by the 14th; refund claims within 12 months and input VAT claims within five years (SECONDARY).

**PAYE and Personal Income Tax, resident individuals, 2026 (VERIFIED, NTA Fourth Schedule; PwC repeats it).** First 800,000 naira at 0%; next 2,200,000 at 15%; next 9,000,000 at 18%; next 13,000,000 at 21%; next 25,000,000 at 23%; above 50,000,000 at 25%. Reliefs (NTA s.30, VERIFIED): National Housing Fund, National Health Insurance Scheme, pension contributions, interest on an owner-occupied home loan, life insurance premiums, and rent relief of 20% of annual rent capped at 500,000; the old Consolidated Relief Allowance is not in the Act (SECONDARY: abolished). Individuals earning the national minimum wage or less are exempt (NTA s.58, VERIFIED; the 70,000 naira figure is SECONDARY). Individuals' chargeable gains go into total income and are taxed at these rates (NTA s.28(2)(a)(v), VERIFIED). Presumptive taxation (NTA s.29 allows assessment by regulation where income cannot be ascertained): the section exists (VERIFIED); no rates or regulation found. Employer annual return of emoluments by 31 January for the preceding year (NTAA s.14, VERIFIED). Deducted tax remitted by the 21st of the following month, penalty 10% a year plus interest at the CBN rate (NTAA s.107(1), VERIFIED). Individual return date: NTAA s.13 requires a return each year but no fixed date was found. 2024 and 2025 under the repealed Personal Income Tax Act: six graduated bands from 7% to 24%, a Consolidated Relief Allowance and a 1% minimum tax (MEMORY, unverified; one search summary reported "7% to 24%"). No mid-year window.

**Companies Income Tax.** 0% for a "small company", 30% for any other company from commencement (VERIFIED, NTA s.56). Small company (gazette-version text): gross turnover of 50 million or less and fixed assets not above 250 million, professional services excluded; the definition differs across versions (below). A presidential reduction to 25% is reported by PwC and others (SECONDARY; not found in the sections read). Development Levy: 4% of assessable profits of all companies other than small and non-resident (NTA s.59, VERIFIED); it replaces (SECONDARY, PwC) the Tertiary Education Tax 3%, NITDA levy 1%, NASENI levy 0.25% and Police Trust Fund levy 0.005%; its proceeds are split 50% to TETFUND and 15% to the Nigerian Education Loan Fund and so on (s.59(3), VERIFIED). Minimum effective tax rate (NTA s.57): where the effective rate is below 15% the company pays additional tax to reach 15%; applies to MNE constituents and to companies with aggregate turnover of 20 billion naira and above in the gazette-version text (PwC, SECONDARY, says 50 billion or a 750 million euro group). Companies' capital gains are included in total profits at the company rate (NTA s.27; rate reasoning SECONDARY). Returns (VERIFIED, NTAA s.11): every company files an annual self-assessment return, even if exempt or not liable, within six months of the accounting year end or, if earlier, 18 months from incorporation or six months after the first accounting period end, with audited accounts (a small company may use a taxpayer-attested statement of accounts), computations and evidence of payment; tax payable on or before the filing date in one sum or instalments (NTAA s.49). Late filing penalty 100,000 naira the first month then 50,000 a month (SECONDARY; NTAA s.101 cited). Late payment penalty 10% of the tax plus interest at the CBN monetary policy rate plus a spread (VERIFIED, NTAA s.65). 2024 and 2025 under the repealed Act (SECONDARY, KPMG Nigeria Fiscal Guide 2023): 30% for turnover over 100 million, 20% for 25 to 100 million, 0% for 25 million or less; minimum tax 0.5% of turnover less franked investment income; Tertiary Education Tax 3% of assessable profit (the KPMG page confirms the bands, minimum tax and six-month filing, not the levies; the levy rates are MEMORY). Whether 2024 and 2025 differed: could not verify.

**Withholding tax.** The payer deducts at the rate in regulations (NTA/NTAA s.51 text, VERIFIED); for dividends, interest, rent, royalty, directors' fees and payments to entertainers and sportspersons, withholding is the final tax for non-resident recipients (VERIFIED). Rates (SECONDARY, PwC reviewed 29 May 2026): dividends and interest 10%; royalty 10% (5% non-corporate); rent 10%; professional, consultancy, technical and management fees 5% resident and 10% non-resident; construction 2% resident and 5% non-resident; other services 2% resident and 5% non-resident. The Withholding Tax Regulations 2024 could not be verified, so when these rates took effect and whether 2026 regulations changed them is unknown. Remitted by the 21st of the following month (VERIFIED, NTAA s.107).

**Payroll-related contributions (all SECONDARY, PwC reviewed 29 May 2026; the Pension Reform Act, NHF and Employees' Compensation Act were not read).** Pension: employers with 15 or more employees; minimum 18% of monthly emolument, 10% employer plus 8% employee (20% if the employer bears all); pension contributions are deductible for the employee (NTA s.30, VERIFIED). National Housing Fund 2.5% of monthly income remitted within one month (compulsory in the public sector, voluntary in the private). NSITF Employee Compensation 1% of payroll, employer-paid (remittance date and penalty vary across sources; unverified). Industrial Training Fund 1% of annual payroll for employers with 5 or more employees or turnover of 50 million or more, paid by 1 April of the following year (SECONDARY, Mondaq and payroll sites; not on PwC's page). National Health Insurance Scheme: could not verify. Whether these changed in 2024 or 2025: not verified per year.

**Other Nigerian taxes.** Stamp duties (VERIFIED, NTA stamp duty schedule): capital duty on nominal shares 0.75%; loan capital 0.125%; contract notes for marketable securities 0.08%; bonds 0.375%; instruments stamped within 30 days (SECONDARY); electronic transfer levy of 50 naira on receipts of 10,000 or more (SECONDARY). Capital gains on shares are not chargeable if proceeds are under 150,000,000 and the gain is not over 10,000,000 in any 12 consecutive months (NTA s.34 proviso, VERIFIED). Personal chattels exempt where consideration does not exceed 5,000,000 or three times the annual minimum wage, whichever is higher (NTA s.52, VERIFIED). Excise changes from 1 July 2026 with scheduled increases in 2027 and 2028, and a 5% fossil fuel surcharge with a commencement date set by the Minister (SECONDARY). State property taxes and land fees at varying rates (SECONDARY). Every taxable person must obtain a Tax ID and banks must require it (NTAA ss.8 to 10, VERIFIED); tax clearance certificate (NTAA s.72, VERIFIED that it exists). Professional-services firms are excluded from both "small company" and "small business".

**Nigeria volatile or disputed (important).** (1) Two textual versions of the Tax Act are in circulation: BusinessDay reports the FIRS-circulated gazette version sets the small company ceiling at 50 million naira and the signed "Final Approved Copy" says 100 million; the NTA and NTAA texts read both say 50 million; a snippet attributed to TheCable (403, not read) reports the NTAA uses 100 million and the presidential tax committee chair said 100 million is correct; PwC says 100 million; KPMG says 50 million: unresolved. (2) Other reported differences between versions (SECONDARY, BusinessDay): the petroleum decommissioning deposit 30% versus 15%; some definitions (financial technology, shared services, labelled startups) absent from the signed copy; the Taxes and Levies (Approved List for Collection) Act listed as repealed in one version only. (3) The minimum effective tax rate threshold: 20 billion naira in the text read versus 50 billion per PwC. (4) A House of Representatives committee was set up to investigate alleged differences between the passed and gazetted texts (allAfrica, SECONDARY); outcome not found. (5) The transition conflict between the NRS (February 2026) and the Minister's Guidelines (18 June 2026). (6) Regulations pending (withholding, presumptive tax, PAYE specifics); the Withholding Tax Regulations 2024 unverified. (7) PAYE and PIT run through state authorities, so filing dates and portals vary by state (example, SECONDARY: Edo State extended its annual PAYE return deadline from 31 January to 14 February 2026). (8) Fossil fuel surcharge and excise commencement depend on ministerial orders.

**Nigeria could not verify.** The signed "Final Approved Copy" of the NTA; the NTA commencement clause text; the Withholding Tax Regulations 2024 and any 2026 regulations; the Pension Reform Act 2014, NHF Act, Employees' Compensation Act and ITF Act texts; NHIS contributions; the Minister's General Transition Guidelines; the individual return due date; old-regime (2024 and 2025) PIT bands, CRA, minimum tax and levy rates (memory or KPMG 2023 secondary only); presumptive tax regulations; the tax-year definition under the repealed Acts.
