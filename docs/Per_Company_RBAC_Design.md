# Per-Company RBAC — Scoping / Design Document

**Status**: scoping and requirements only, 2026-09-23. No code written. Grounded directly against real code in `EA` and `WEB` (file/class citations throughout) — nothing invented, nothing assumed. Commissioned as the resolved dependency named in `FiSH/docs/Unified_Communication_SRS.md` §7 Q3: FR-COMM-04's build (T3–T5) is sequenced to start only after the work scoped here lands.

---

## 1. Problem Statement

### 1.1 The requirement, as elicited from the user

Today, one `Membership` (the join of `User` × `Tenant` × `Role`) carries exactly **one** `Role` and **one** `AccessLevel` for the *entire* set of Companies it has access to under a Tenant. The user's stated requirement, across a live conversation, is that this is wrong for ordinary staff:

1. **Every staff member must have RBAC scoped to a specific Company** — "Every staff must have RBAC which means he belongs to a company. You cannot be in the FiSH+ERVerse without having a role in an organisation." Concretely: a `Membership`'s current `companyIds = null` state ("unrestricted — every Company under the Tenant") is **illegitimate for ordinary staff**. That state is reserved for the Owner-Admin alone.
2. **Staff can legitimately hold access at more than one Company** under the same Tenant — "The same staff can be invited to hold roles across a tenancy."
3. **Those roles can genuinely differ per Company** — not the same `Role` replicated across every Company in scope. The user's own example: a Manager based in Brighton also holds accountancy skills usable at a London Company under the same Tenant — the same person is "Manager" at one Company and "Accountant" at another, not one `Role` value applied to both.
4. **The invite flow *is* the Company-Role assignment, not an optional refinement of it** — the user corrected an earlier framing that treated Company scoping as an add-on to "joining the Tenant": "What is the person invited to do? There must be a role to handle within an organisation." The invite is fundamentally "a Role, at a Company," not "membership in a Tenant, optionally scoped to a Company."
5. **The Owner-Admin is the one legitimate exception** — `Tenant.businessOwnerId`, exactly one `User` per Tenant (already confirmed and further narrowed to exactly one `Role.OWNER_ADMIN` Membership per Tenant, per `Unified_Communication_SRS.md` §7 Q1, resolved 2026-09-23). The Owner-Admin sits above every Company under their Tenant by definition and is exempt from the per-Company requirement above.

### 1.2 What's actually built today (grounded)

- `EA/src/main/kotlin/com/theprodeogroup/ea/domain/tenancy/membership.kt` — the `Membership` aggregate has exactly one `role: Role` and one `accessLevel: AccessLevel` field (constructor, lines 58–66), plus a single `companyIds: Set<CompanyId>?` (line 73). `null` means unrestricted access to *every* Company under the Tenant, including ones added later (KDoc lines 39–56); a non-null set restricts access to exactly those Companies, but the **same** `role`/`accessLevel` values apply uniformly across every Company in that set. There is no per-Company variation possible in this shape at all.
- `hasAccessToCompany(companyId)` (line 116) only answers "is this Company in scope," never "what Role applies at this Company."
- `restrictToCompanies()`/`removeCompanyRestriction()` (lines 119–130) only ever mutate the Company *set*, never anything role-shaped per Company.
- The aggregate's own KDoc (lines 39–56) already flags this as a known, deliberate gap from the 2026-09-06 `companyIds` addition: *"Building this into the Membership model is this backlog item's whole scope — actually enforcing it... is deliberately not done here."* Confirmed still true (§2.3 below) — no production authorization path anywhere calls `hasAccessToCompany()` today.
- `InviteStaffMemberUseCase.Request` (`EA/src/main/kotlin/com/theprodeogroup/ea/application/InviteStaffMemberUseCase.kt`, lines 70–89) takes one `role: Role` and one optional `companyIds: Set<CompanyId>?` — its own KDoc (lines 55–59) states plainly: *"`null` (the default) grants the pre-existing behavior, access to every Company under the Tenant; a non-null set scopes the new Membership to just those"* — one Role, N Companies, never N (Role, Company) pairs.

This is a real, structural shape gap, not a missing validation rule — no amount of extra checking against the current `Membership`/`MembershipsTable`/`InviteStaffMemberUseCase.Request` shape can represent "Manager at Company A, Accountant at Company B" for one person. The fields have to change.

## 2. Blast Radius

Organized by layer. Each item states the current behavior (with citation) and precisely what assumption breaks.

### 2.1 Domain (`EA/src/main/kotlin/com/theprodeogroup/ea/domain/tenancy/`)

| File / member | Current assumption | What breaks |
|---|---|---|
| `membership.kt` — `Membership` class (lines 58–66) | One `role: Role`, one `accessLevel: AccessLevel` per Membership, independent of `companyIds`. | The aggregate has no way to associate a distinct Role/AccessLevel with each Company in `companyIds`. This is the root of the whole blast radius — every other item below either reads these two fields directly or is downstream of something that does. |
| `membership.kt` — `grant()`/`invite()`/`reconstitute()` factories (lines 162–213) | Single `role`, `accessLevel`, `companyIds` parameters. | Cannot express "grant Role X at Company A and Role Y at Company B" in one call. Either becomes multi-valued, or the aggregate needs a way to add/change a per-Company assignment after construction (mirroring how `restrictToCompanies()` already mutates state post-construction). |
| `membership.kt` — `hasAccessToCompany()` (line 116) | Answers "in scope or not," never "what Role." | Every caller that currently uses this purely as a scope gate (none exist in production yet — see 2.3) will need a Role-aware sibling, e.g. "what Role/AccessLevel applies at Company X," not just a boolean. |
| `membership.kt` — `restrictToCompanies()` / `removeCompanyRestriction()` (lines 118–130) | Company set membership is orthogonal to Role; changing the set never touches Role. | If Role becomes per-Company, restricting/expanding the Company set now has to decide what Role (if any) a newly-added Company gets — this can no longer be a bare set operation. |
| `role.kt` — `Role` enum | A label enum, Tenant-scoped by construction (used as `Membership.role`, one per Membership). | No change to the enum's *values* is implied, but every *use* of `Role` as "the one Role" (return types, comparisons like `callerMembership.role != Role.OWNER_ADMIN` in `TenantRoutes.kt` line 192) needs to be re-examined: is "the Role" now always qualified by a Company, except for `OWNER_ADMIN`? |
| `access_level.kt` — `AccessLevel` enum + `atLeast()` | Declared as "the core enforcement primitive... an operative can be granted `WRITE` without `APPROVE`" — written with a Tenant-wide mental model, KDoc doesn't mention Company scope at all. | Whether `AccessLevel` needs the same per-Company treatment as `Role` is itself unresolved — see Open Question (d). |
| `tenant.kt` — `Tenant.businessOwnerId` (lines 32–51) | Exactly one `User` per Tenant, set once at `onboard()`, exempt from Company scoping by definition. | No change needed to `Tenant` itself — this is the one identity this whole redesign explicitly does *not* apply to. But every authorization/UI change below needs to keep checking "is this the Owner-Admin" as an escape hatch from the new per-Company requirement, not just check Role/AccessLevel generically. |

### 2.2 Persistence (`EA/src/main/kotlin/com/theprodeogroup/ea/infrastructure/persistence/`)

| File / table | Current assumption | What breaks |
|---|---|---|
| `tenancy_tables.kt` — `MembershipsTable` (lines 78–88) | `role` and `access_level` are single columns on the `memberships` row itself — one value per Membership, full stop. | A single-row-per-Membership `role`/`access_level` column structurally cannot hold N (Company, Role, AccessLevel) tuples. This table's shape has to change — either the columns move off `memberships` entirely into a new join table, or `memberships` stops being the place per-Company Role lives (exact shape is explicitly out of scope for this document — flagged as the first task in §4). |
| `tenancy_tables.kt` — `MembershipCompanyGrantsTable` (lines 105–110) | Deliberately just `(membershipId, companyId)` — presence/absence only, no Role/AccessLevel columns at all. It's the natural candidate to be widened (add `role`/`access_level` columns) or replaced by an equivalent per-Company-role table — again, exact shape is a follow-on task, not decided here. |
| `exposed_membership_repository.kt` — `ExposedMembershipRepository.populate()` (lines 80–86) and `toMembership()` (lines 88–109) | Writes/reads exactly one `role`/`accessLevel` per Membership row; separately loads the `companyIds` set with no Role association (line 94–98). | Both directions of this mapping need to change: `save()` needs to persist N per-Company assignments, not one row's worth of `role`/`access_level`; `toMembership()`/`reconstitute()` need to reconstruct a Membership whose Role varies by Company. |

### 2.3 Web / Authorization gates (`EA/src/main/kotlin/com/theprodeogroup/ea/infrastructure/web/Auth.kt`)

All four authorization functions take a `tenantId` only — none take a `companyId`, and none check `hasAccessToCompany()`:

- `authorizeTenantForWrite(tenantId)` (lines 237–253) — resolves `caller.memberships.firstOrNull { it.tenantId == tenantId }`, then checks `membership.accessLevel.atLeast(AccessLevel.WRITE)` as a single value.
- `authorizeTenantForAdmin(tenantId)` (lines 260–276) — same pattern, `AccessLevel.ADMIN`.
- `authorizeTenantForModule(tenantId, module, minAccessLevel)` (lines 282–302) — same pattern, plus a `module in membership.grantedModules` check (Tenant-wide, not Company-scoped — see §2.6 below on `grantedModules`).
- `authorizeTenantForRead(tenantId)` (lines 309–325) — same pattern, `AccessLevel.READ`.

**What breaks**: none of these four gates has any notion of "for which Company." Once a Membership's Role/AccessLevel is per-Company, every one of these needs a `companyId` parameter (where the underlying route acts on a specific Company) and needs to resolve the caller's Role/AccessLevel *at that Company*, not a single Tenant-wide value — except for the Owner-Admin escape hatch (§1.1 point 5), which should presumably still pass regardless of Company.

**A pre-existing, related finding surfaced by this research** (not previously enforced, confirmed by grep — no production caller of `hasAccessToCompany()` exists today): the `companyIds` restriction added 2026-09-06 was **already** never actually enforced at the authorization-gate level — `Membership`'s own KDoc says as much (§2.1 above). This means today, a Membership restricted to Company A can still, in practice, call any `authorizeTenantFor*`-gated route that only checks Tenant-level `accessLevel`/`grantedModules`, with no code path stopping it from acting against Company B's data (where the route itself resolves a Company, e.g. via GL's `resolveTenantForCompany`, that check happens in GL, not EA — see §2.7). This restructuring doesn't introduce this gap; it inherits it, and closing it becomes unavoidable once Role becomes genuinely Company-specific (§4 Tier 2).

### 2.5 Web routes (`EA/src/main/kotlin/com/theprodeogroup/ea/infrastructure/web/TenantRoutes.kt`)

- `POST /tenants/{tenantId}/memberships` (lines 172–242, the invite route): parses one `role` (line 185), applies an OWNER_ADMIN-invite guard that reads `callerMembership.role != Role.OWNER_ADMIN` as a single Tenant-wide comparison (lines 190–196 — this one is fine to keep Tenant-wide per §1.1 point 5, since OWNER_ADMIN itself stays a Tenant-wide, Company-exempt concept), parses a flat `companyIds` list (lines 203–208), and calls the use case with both as a single pair (lines 210–221). The response (lines 227–240) echoes `result.membership.role.name` singular (line 233).
- `GET /tenants/{tenantId}/memberships` (lines 244–270, the staff list route): builds `MembershipDto` per Membership with `role = membership.role.name` singular (line 263) — this is exactly what a per-Company roster needs to stop doing.
- `PATCH .../modules` route (lines 279–334): see §2.4 above, same Tenant-wide shape, orthogonal field.

**What breaks**: every one of these three routes' request parsing, use-case call, and response shaping assumes a single Role value round-trips cleanly. All three need to change together, since they share the same `MembershipDto`/`InviteStaffMemberRequestDto`/`InviteStaffMemberResponseDto` shapes (§2.4).

### 2.6 `GET /me` and its response shape (`MeRoutes.kt`, `Dtos.kt`)

- `MeRoutes.kt` (lines 24–66): for each of the caller's Memberships, builds one `MyTenantDto` per Tenant with `role = membership.role.name` and `accessLevel = membership.accessLevel.name` as single top-level strings (lines 44–45).
- `Dtos.kt` — `MyTenantDto` (lines 78–91): `role: String`, `accessLevel: String` fields, both singular, alongside a `companies: List<CompanySummaryDto>` list that is **not** filtered by the Membership's own `companyIds` restriction at all — it's built from `tenant.companyIds` (every Company under the Tenant, `MeRoutes.kt` line 34/38), not `membership.companyIds` (the caller's actual restricted scope). This is a related, pre-existing gap surfaced by this research, not something the user asked about directly: today, a restricted Membership's `/me` response already shows every Company under the Tenant regardless of restriction, only the singular `role`/`accessLevel` describing access that (per the domain model) doesn't uniformly apply. Once Role is genuinely per-Company, this gap becomes actively misleading rather than just incomplete — the frontend would show the person as having a Role at Companies they may have no access to at all.

**What breaks**: `MyTenantDto` is what every downstream WEB screen reads (§2.8) — this is the single highest-leverage change point. It needs to become something like a per-Company breakdown (Company × Role × AccessLevel) rather than one flat Role/AccessLevel string pair, and needs to actually filter `companies` by the Membership's real scope while it's being touched anyway.

### 2.7 Sibling services' own EA-membership mirrors (GL, POP, SOP, IM)

Not explicitly named in the elicitation, but a real, confirmed part of the blast radius: `GL`, `POP`, `SOP`, and `IM` each hand-mirror a subset of EA's `GET /me` shape as their own local `CallerMembership` type, per the code-review-driven rollout documented in the top-level `CLAUDE.md` ("EA's proven `GET /me`/`EaMembershipGateway` pattern was piloted on `IM` then generalized to `POP` and `SOP`").

- `GL/src/main/kotlin/com/theprodeogroup/fish/infrastructure/ea/ea_membership_gateway.kt` (lines 21–32) — `CallerMembership` carries `role: Role` and `accessLevel: AccessLevel` as single fields, explicitly documented as mirroring `MyTenantDto` "field-for-field" (KDoc lines 8–19). GL's own `resolveTenantForCompany(companyId, companyRepository)` (`GL/.../infrastructure/web/Auth.kt` lines 459–466) resolves *which Tenant* owns a Company, then authorizes at the Tenant level only (`authorizeTenantForWrite(tenantId)` etc.) — it never checks whether the caller's Role/AccessLevel is specific to *that* Company. This pattern repeats across at least 20 GL route files that call `resolveTenantForCompany` (`JournalEntryRoutes.kt`, `PurchasePostingContextRoutes.kt`, `SalesPostingContextRoutes.kt`, `InventoryPostingContextRoutes.kt`, `PayrollPostingContextRoutes.kt`, `FixedAssetRoutes.kt`, and others) — every one of GL's actual posting/reporting routes authorizes "does this Membership have WRITE somewhere in this Tenant," never "does this Membership have WRITE *at this specific Company*."
- `IM/src/main/kotlin/com/theprodeogroup/im/infrastructure/ea/ea_membership_gateway.kt` (lines 9–13) — IM's `CallerMembership` is narrower still: `accessLevel` and `grantedModules` only, **no `role` field at all** and no `companyIds`. IM is single-company-per-deployment (`ImMembershipAuthorizer`'s own KDoc, `IM/.../infrastructure/web/Auth.kt` lines 212–226: "IM has no `tenantId` anywhere in its routes at all... single company, no multi-tenant scoping"), so it is structurally insulated from this specific change today, but if IM is ever deployed multi-company per Tenant, this same gap would need closing.
- `POP/src/main/kotlin/com/theprodeogroup/pop/infrastructure/ea/ea_membership_gateway.kt` follows the same single-company-per-deployment pattern as IM's (`PopMembershipAuthorizer`, `POP_GL_ENGINE_COMPANY_ID`).
- `SOP/src/main/kotlin/com/theprodeogroup/sop/infrastructure/ea/ea_membership_gateway.kt` — **corrected 2026-09-23, this document's own earlier claim that SOP "follows the same pattern as IM's" was wrong.** SOP is *not* single-company-per-deployment: `companyId` is a genuine per-request field on several of its own routes (`SalesOrderRoutes.kt`'s record-sale/record-collection, `OrdinarySaleRoutes.kt`, `ReturnRequestRoutes.kt`), discovered while fixing SOP's own `GET /me` compatibility break (below). `authorizeSopForWrite`/`ForRead` run before the request body is parsed, so there is no Company in scope to check against at authorization time - `SopMembershipAuthorizer`'s own pre-existing KDoc already documented this as "Membership is Tenant-scoped only, never Company-scoped," a real, live gap this document's per-Company RBAC work does not yet close for SOP. The compatibility fix (below) deliberately did not attempt to close it - see that entry for why, and for the concrete blast radius: **any Membership with WRITE at even one Company under a Tenant currently gets WRITE against every Company under that Tenant via SOP's own routes**, unchanged behavior from before this whole redesign (not a regression this rewrite introduced), but a real, tracked gap now that per-Company RBAC exists everywhere else and SOP is the one service that doesn't yet enforce it.

  **Closed (partially, by design), 2026-09-23, `fix/sop-per-company-write-scoping`, 31d4e4d.** Investigated all 8 of SOP's own route files, not just the two named above - only 4 routes across 2 files genuinely carry a `companyId` at all (`record-sale`/`record-collection` in `SalesOrderRoutes.kt`, `/sales` in `OrdinarySaleRoutes.kt`, `issue-credit-note` in `ReturnRequestRoutes.kt` - all four GL-posting routes, where `companyId` names the Company the resulting journal entry posts against). Every other SOP route (`Customer`, `ReturnRequest`, `CustomerComplaint`, `CreditNote`'s own CRUD/lifecycle endpoints, `InventoryAvailabilityRoutes.kt`) has **no Company concept on the domain object at all** - `Customer`/`ReturnRequest`/`CreditNote` don't carry a `companyId` field anywhere today, so there is nothing to scope those routes to without first deciding whether those aggregates should carry one - a real domain-modeling question, not an auth-wiring one, deliberately not answered here. `SopMembershipAuthorizer.resolve()` gained an optional `companyId` parameter; the four GL-posting routes now parse their request body (where `companyId` lives) before authorizing, and call the new `authorizeSopForWriteAt(companyId)` instead of the Tenant-wide `authorizeSopForWrite()`. Everything else keeps using the Tenant-wide check, correctly, not as an oversight. 265 tests pass (264 existing + 1 new regression proving the scoping is real: a Membership with ADMIN access at a different Company gets 403 on `record-sale` targeting the request's own Company).

**What breaks**: this restructuring's real authorization consequence (GL's own routes gate on a Company-scoped resource but authorize only Tenant-wide) is arguably the single biggest hidden piece of this blast radius — it's not just an EA-internal model change, it's a live gap in GL's own production authorization today, which per-Company Role makes structurally impossible to ignore once EA can actually report *which* Role applies at which Company. Whether GL (and eventually POP/SOP/IM, once/if they become multi-company) gets rewired to consume that per-Company data is squarely in scope for the task list (§4) even though the elicitation didn't name GL's own auth code directly.

## 3. Open Design Questions — not resolved here

Recorded per this project's own "park, don't guess" convention. None of these are answered by this document.

**(a) Does `grantedModules` also need to become per-Company, or stay Tenant-wide?**
**RESOLVED, 2026-09-23, direct instruction: per Company.** `grantedModules` goes per-Company alongside `Role`/`AccessLevel`, not left Tenant-wide — consistent, not the split model this question flagged as a risk. `UpdateMembershipModulesUseCase`/`PATCH .../modules` (§2.4, Task 11) is a real rewrite, not a no-op.

**(b) What happens to an existing Membership's data during migration?**
**RESOLVED, 2026-09-23** — via a discovery that reshapes `Role` itself, not just migration. Working through this question surfaced that the current `Role` enum (`OWNER_ADMIN`, `ACCOUNTANT`, `APPROVER`, `READ_ONLY`, `COMPLIANCE_ETHICS_REVIEW`, pulled from the spec's RBAC baseline §7.8) mixes two genuinely different things: real organisational job functions, and permission-tier/oversight concepts that don't belong in `Role` at all. Resolved as follows:

- **`APPROVER` and `READ_ONLY` are retired from `Role` entirely** — direct instruction: "These are not roles. They are functions - What roles can do." Both are already redundant with `AccessLevel.APPROVE`/`AccessLevel.READ` (`access_level.kt`), which already exists and needs no change ("This is enough").
- **`COMPLIANCE_ETHICS_REVIEW` is dropped entirely for now** — a third concept ("Functions": discrete capabilities like this one, independent of Role/AccessLevel) was considered and explicitly declined: "I do not think we need this at this stage." Not migrated to anything; if Purse needs it later, that's a future, separate decision.
- **`Role` becomes six real organisational functions, confirmed as existing "in every organisation... regardless of size"**: `OWNER_ADMIN`, `ACCOUNTANT`, `SALES_OFFICER`, `PURCHASING_OFFICER`, `INVENTORY_MANAGER`, `HR_OFFICER`. Five of these map 1:1 onto the existing `ManagedModule` enum (Sales→SOP, Purchasing→POP, Inventory→IM, Accountant→GL/TAX, HR→HR); `OWNER_ADMIN` stays the Tenant-wide, Company-exempt concept already established.
- **Migration strategy, resolved by this same mapping**: derive each Company's starting Role(s) from that Membership's *existing* `grantedModules` data, rather than either inventing an unfounded fact or leaving every current staff member locked out. A Membership with SOP access becomes Sales Officer at that Company; GL access becomes Accountant; and so on — a real, grounded starting point from data that already exists, not a guess and not a blank slate.
- **The "unrestricted, every Company" `null` state**: still to be forcibly retired for every non-Owner-Admin Membership per requirement 1.1 point 1 — the migration above is exactly the mechanism that retires it, by deriving concrete per-Company Role/module facts to replace it.

**New sub-question surfaced, not yet resolved**: industry-aware Role *labels*. Confirmed directly: `Role.SALES_OFFICER` should display as "Admissions Officer" when the Company's `IndustryType == SCHOOL` — a presentation-layer relabeling, not a structural change (the enum value, module linkage, and underlying permission stay identical; only the display text varies by industry). Rich domain context supplied for whenever this is built: the Admissions Officer "works in the school's registry run by the Registrar" and "ensure[s] that the delivery of the contract (i.e. the contract to educate person(s)) is fulfilled" — this points directly at the still-unresolved EA/SchoolAdmissions identity-bridging question (`Unified_Communication_SRS.md` §7 Q2, the Registrar being a `StaffAssignment` role on the SchoolAdmissions side, not an EA Membership), but is explicitly scoped here as a display-label concern only, not an attempt to resolve that bridge. Where exactly this label mapping lives (a static per-IndustryType lookup table? Something WEB-side only?) is not decided — flagged as a new task in §4, not designed further here.

**2026-09-23 addendum — this sub-question is more than a lookup table.** Asked whether the other four functional Roles need the same industry-specific relabeling treatment: "May be, maybe not. For example, if we create another industry type such as a Doctors surgery, the SOP may be the Pharmacy (Goods) and the Doctor's Consultancy (Services) but the appropriate moniker will not be Sales." Then, elaborating on the underlying accounting reality: "Income Generation in traditional accounting can be sales of physical goods, manufacturing, or service provision." This reframes the question: a Doctor's-surgery-style industry doesn't just need a different *label* for `Role.SALES_OFFICER` — it potentially needs `SOP` itself to represent **two distinct income-generation channels within one industry** (goods vs. services), which is a domain-modeling question about `SOP`'s own shape, not a presentation-layer relabeling at all. **Not resolved here** — flagged as its own open question, separate from (and likely blocking) any attempt to build a generic per-`IndustryType` Role-label table for the four non-`SALES_OFFICER` roles. No answer invented for Purchasing Officer/Inventory Manager/Accountant/HR Officer's own school-equivalent (or any other industry's) labels — genuinely still open, not defaulted.

**(c) Can a Membership legitimately have a Role at Company A but no Role at Company B?**
I.e., is "this Membership can see Company B" itself optional independent of having a Role there, or does being in scope for a Company *require* a Role for that Company (making "scope" and "has a Role here" the same fact, not two independent ones)? Today's model treats `companyIds` (scope) and `role` (the one Role) as two independent fields; per-Company Role could collapse them into one ("the set of Companies this Membership has a Role at, and what that Role is, per Company") — but that is a real design fork with implementation consequences (e.g., can `restrictToCompanies()` still exist as a bare set operation, or does adding a Company to scope now require supplying a Role in the same call?). Not decided here.

**(d) Does `AccessLevel` need the same per-Company treatment as `Role`, or can it stay Tenant-wide while only `Role` goes per-Company?**
**RESOLVED, 2026-09-23, direct instruction: "Per Company."** `AccessLevel` goes per-Company alongside `Role` and `grantedModules` (§3(a)) — all three axes of what a Membership can do at a given Company now vary together, per Company, not just the job label. This confirms §2.3's authorization-gate rewrite (Task 9) means "resolve Role *and* AccessLevel *and* grantedModules per Company for real enforcement," not a display-only Role change — the highest-impact resolution in this document, exactly as flagged.

Direct consequence for §2.8/Task 20: `TenantDashboard.tsx` line 310's `canBroadcast={myTenant.accessLevel === 'ADMIN'}` needs revisiting now that `AccessLevel` is per-Company — but broadcasting (`MessageAudience.Everyone`, `Unified_Communication_SRS.md` FR-COMM-03) is a genuinely Tenant-wide action, not tied to any one Company. Whether "ADMIN somewhere" or "ADMIN at every Company" (or something else) gates a Tenant-wide action once AccessLevel is Company-scoped is a new, small open question this resolution creates — not decided here, flagged for whoever implements Task 20.

**(e) Does inviting someone to a new Company under an existing Membership go through the same `InviteStaffMemberUseCase`/invite-and-accept flow, or does it need its own use case?**
**RESOLVED, 2026-09-23, direct instruction: applies immediately, no new acceptance step.** "Just add the necessary access levels that he needs to function in that role" — an already-ACTIVE, trusted Membership gaining a new Company-Role assignment doesn't re-enter PENDING or need a fresh accept step; the new (Company, Role, AccessLevel, modules) tuple is simply added. Today's "already ACTIVE = no-op" branch (`InviteStaffMemberUseCase.execute()`, lines 122–123) still needs to change — a no-op is wrong once there's a new Company being added — but the fix is "apply the new assignment directly," not "start a second acceptance flow."

**(f) Does the Owner-Admin's Company-exemption need to be encoded structurally, or just checked as a special case at each authorization site?**
**RESOLVED, 2026-09-23, direct instruction: its own distinct type.** But with a real, load-bearing nuance the original framing of this question missed: being Owner-Admin does **not** mean bypassing every operational AccessLevel check — direct instruction: "Because he is the owner does not necessarily give the right to amend accounting records especially when he has an accountant doing that." Owner-Admin status grants *ownership-level* capabilities that need no per-Company assignment at all (oversight of every Company, approving cross-Company/cross-Tenant communication per `Unified_Communication_SRS.md` FR-COMM-04/05, staff/invite management) — but it does **not** automatically grant operational Role/AccessLevel permissions (e.g. WRITE on GL journal entries) in a domain a specific Role has actually been assigned to. If the Owner-Admin wants hands-on operational access somewhere, they need their own explicit (Company, Role, AccessLevel) assignment there too, exactly like ordinary staff — ownership alone doesn't override separation of duties.

**One more confirmed nuance**: unlike ordinary staff, who can only be granted a (Company, Role, AccessLevel) assignment by someone else (the invite flow), the Owner-Admin can **self-assign** any AccessLevel at any of their own Companies — "The Owner admin can give himself whatever access levels he wants across his companies." This is consistent with, not a contradiction of, the "no automatic ADMIN everywhere" resolution above: the Owner-Admin still doesn't have operational access *by default*, but uniquely among Memberships, they have the standing authority to grant it to themselves at will, rather than needing to be invited/assigned by anyone else.

**Consequence for Task 9 (Auth.kt rewrite)**: "the Owner-Admin exemption applied first" is not a blanket bypass. The distinct Owner-Admin type needs two separate things modeled: (1) intrinsic owner-level capabilities requiring no per-Company data at all — **including a floor of `AccessLevel.READ` at every Company, confirmed directly** ("He definitely will have read access") — the Owner-Admin can always *view* everything, oversight is never gated; and (2) whatever ordinary per-Company Role/AccessLevel/`grantedModules` assignments the Owner-Admin *also* happens to hold, resolved exactly the same way as any other Membership's, if they want WRITE/APPROVE/ADMIN-level operational access somewhere. So the intrinsic floor is specifically `READ`, not silence and not full `ADMIN` — anything above `READ` in a given domain still requires an explicit assignment, same as ordinary staff. Exact type shape (a distinct `OwnerAdminMembership`? a Membership-with-a-flag plus the same per-Company assignment collection everyone else has?) is left to the next design pass, not decided here — but "Owner-Admin implies ADMIN everywhere" is now confirmed wrong and should not be built.

**(g) Does `MembershipCompanyGrantsTable`'s current "no rows = unrestricted" convention (§2.2) survive at all once ordinary-staff `null`/unrestricted is retired (point 1.1.1)?**
**RESOLVED, 2026-09-23: retired outright.** Direct confirmation: "There is no need to see a company if you have nothing to do there." Combined with (c) (scope and Role collapse into one fact) and (f) (the Owner-Admin is its own distinct type, not a Membership-with-empty-grants), there is no remaining case where "zero rows" should mean "unrestricted" — every ordinary staff Membership will always have explicit rows (one per Company/Role held), and a Membership with zero rows going forward means exactly what it looks like: no access anywhere, not a signal to interpret specially. The next design pass (Task 4, persistence shape) should not carry this convention forward.

**Implementation correction to (f)/(g), 2026-09-23**: `CompanyRoleAssignment` was first built (Tier 2) banning `Role.OWNER_ADMIN` outright, on the theory that the Owner-Admin's own capabilities live entirely on `Membership.isOwnerAdmin` and never as a per-Company assignment. That turned out to be wrong once `RegisterCompanyUseCase`'s own onboarding-lockout fix (below) needed to give the Owner-Admin real operational access at a newly-registered Company: forcing that grant to masquerade as one of the five ordinary functional Roles (e.g. labeling the owner "Accountant" when they're just exercising ownership directly) would misrepresent what's happening. Revised: `Role.OWNER_ADMIN` **is** a legal `CompanyRoleAssignment` value, gated so only a Membership with `isOwnerAdmin = true` may ever hold one (enforced in `Membership.assignAt`, not in `CompanyRoleAssignment` itself) — reads as "the owner, acting personally, not through a delegated function."

**(h) New question, surfaced during Tier 3 implementation, not one of the original (a)–(g): who besides the Owner-Admin can manage staff (invite / list the roster / re-permission modules) once there's no more Tenant-wide `AccessLevel.ADMIN` to fall back to?**
**RESOLVED, 2026-09-23.** First cut made this Owner-Admin-exclusive (the simplest reading of "there's no Tenant-wide ADMIN concept left"). Flagged back for confirmation before shipping (a WEB peer session's own review caught the tightening), and corrected: "Methinks you will still need this or some form of this e.g. if the owner admin has HR being managed for him." Resolved as delegation tied to the **HR_OFFICER function specifically**, not a generic "ADMIN access level at any Company" bypass — staff records are HR's domain by definition. Concretely: `Membership.canManageStaffAt(companyId)` is true for the Owner-Admin (Tenant-wide) or for any Membership holding a `Role.HR_OFFICER` assignment at `AccessLevel.ADMIN` at that specific Company. An HR-delegate caller is scoped to companies they themselves HR-administer — they cannot use their standing at Company A to invite or list staff at Company B, and the invite route checks this per-assignment (a single invite call can still target several Companies, but every one of them must be a company the caller can manage staff at). Implemented in `Auth.kt` (`authorizeStaffManagement`) and `TenantRoutes.kt`'s invite/list/modules-patch routes.
